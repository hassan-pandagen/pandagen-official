### Group B — the control group

**Every other page on the site is unchanged.** That is roughly 88 audited pages, most of
them blog posts. They are the control.

If Group A and Group B move together after deploy, the cause is not these edits — it is an
algorithmic change, a seasonal effect, or the existing down-trend continuing. Only a
divergence between the two groups is evidence that the edits did anything.

Do not skip this comparison. A sitewide move read as a content effect is the most common
way this kind of measurement goes wrong.

## 4. GSC comparison plan

Pull at page level, not site level.

- **Windows:** 28 days before the deploy date vs 28 days after. Also compare each against
  the same 28 days in the prior year, to separate the edits from seasonality and from the
  existing down-trend.
- **Metrics, per URL:** clicks, impressions, average position, CTR. Keep impressions and
  position separate from clicks — a CTR change with flat impressions means the snippet
  changed; an impressions change means ranking or coverage changed.
- **Lag:** GSC data is 2–3 days behind. The "after" window does not start until the deploy
  date plus the time Google takes to recrawl each page, which is not uniform. Check
  **Last crawl date** in URL Inspection per page before treating that page's after-window
  as valid.
- **Known confounders already on the table, do not attribute these to the edits:**
  - The existing GSC down-trend, which predates all of this work.
  - The Desktop 53% / Smartphone 7% crawl split anomaly, still unexplained.
  - The August 2026 spam update, which completed before this window but whose effects
    may still be settling.
- **Noise floor:** traffic is low. Week-to-week differences of a handful of clicks are not
  signal. Do not call a result before the full 28 days.

headings when selecting title links. If clicks fall over the next 28 days, this is the first
place to look, and the control group is how to tell.

## 6. Lock rule

From the deploy date, the pages in Group A are **frozen for the full 28-day after-window**
except for factual corrections.

Rationale: each redeploy restarts the recrawl clock and contaminates the window. A page
edited three times in a fortnight cannot be measured, and repeated churn on a URL is not
a neutral act with the crawler.

The exception is deliberate and narrow: **a false claim gets fixed immediately, measurement
window or not.** Accuracy outranks attribution. Log any such fix in section 5 with its date
so the window can be split around it.

Structural work — the homepage reduction from 4,098 words, the H1 repositioning, the
inner-page headline edits — waits for the after-window to close.

months, 2026-06-12 to 2026-09-11. Compared against the 25 Aug, 29 Aug and 4 Sep exports

### 8.1 The site is not down-trending

Rolling 3-month window, 25 August export vs 13 September export, 19 days apart:

| | 25 Aug | 13 Sep | change |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Clicks | 243 | 259 | **+6.6%** |
| Impressions | 141,968 | 159,506 | **+12.4%** |
| Clicks excluding the outlier page | 224 | 235 | **+4.9%** |
| Impressions excluding the outlier page | 51,496 | 58,472 | **+13.5%** |

Every significant click-earning page grew: `divi-theme-slow` 31→36, `gohighlevel-website-speed`
29→34, `nextjs-hosting-zero-cost` 19→24, `woocommerce-too-slow` 20→21.

What is true is that the most recent fortnight is below the peak fortnight. Clicks per
14 days across the window: 33, 36, 41, 45, **50** (7–20 Aug), 39, then ~26 projected from
the final 8 days. That is a fall from a spike, not a trend reversal, and the last 2–3 days
of any GSC export are incomplete, which drags the final bucket down artificially. At 1–4
clicks a day the noise floor is larger than the movement.

Average position did drop hard in one window — 10.5 to **16.4** across 21 Aug–3 Sep — then
recovered to 11.1. The August 2026 spam update ran 18–20 August (`src/data/google-updates.ts`).
The timing lines up. Treat it as a correlation worth watching, not an established cause:
average position is a composite that moves whenever the query mix changes.

### 8.2 The real finding: the site ranks for questions its buyers do not ask

| | Pages | Clicks | Impressions | CTR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Blog | 98 | 195 | 153,898 | 0.13% |
| Commercial (home, services, pricing, work, contact) | 44 | 64 | 5,608 | **1.14%** |

Commercial pages convert impressions to clicks **8.8× better** than blog pages, and receive
**3.5%** of the impressions.

One page distorts everything. `/blog/nextjs-hosting-zero-cost` holds **101,034 impressions —
63% of the entire site — and 24 clicks. CTR 0.02% at average position 8.5.** Its queries are
"vercel free tier bandwidth 100gb" and about twenty near-identical variants. The top 25
queries by impression produce **13,665 impressions and one click**.

The Generative AI Features export explains it: that export carries impressions and no clicks
at all, and this page is 5,277 of 9,142 of them. The page ranks, is consumed by AI Overviews
answering a factual lookup, and the searcher never needs to click. It is also why desktop is
**89.7% of impressions** — developers researching Vercel pricing, not owners looking for a
website.

### 8.3 What this means for the audit's recommendations

The homepage earns **50 clicks from 1,032 impressions: 4.84% CTR at position 8.1.** It is the
single largest click source on the site and it converts 30× better than the site average. It
is not failing to persuade. It is barely being shown.

`/pricing` received **61 impressions and 0 clicks in 92 days.** `/work` received 42. Every
work case page is in single or low double digits.

So the pages the audit recommends restructuring are pages search traffic does not currently
reach. Restructuring them cannot move the business until they have impressions. Combined with
the auditor's own 13 September retractions — no validated word-count target, the 6.5/10 is an
informal opinion with no rubric, the generic H1 was an unproven positioning hypothesis — the
case for a homepage restructure as the next priority does not hold on this evidence.

The commercial-intent queries that should work are stuck outside the top 10:
`website migration cost` at position 14.1 (428 impressions, 1 click);
`/services/wordpress-migration` at 15.0; `/contact` at 34.3.

## 9. Why there were no enquiries — found 13 September

**Both lead-capture forms returned HTTP 503 in production from launch until 8 September 2026.**

- The homepage quote form posts to `/api/submit-quote` (`HeroLeadForm.tsx:109`).
- `redisConfig()` threw whenever the Redis URL/token pair was incomplete, and that throw
  reached the route handler. Redis was never configured on this project.
- `privateKey()` then threw the same error class a line later, because
  `AUDIT_RATE_LIMIT_SECRET` was never configured either.
- The free audit tool failed the same way.

The only commit that removed the throw and the "temporarily unavailable" response is
`39d3c5e`, dated **8 September 2026**.

**CONTRADICTED 14 September, and this correction matters.** The claim above came from a
code comment, not from delivery evidence. The Resend sending log, read on 14 September,
shows a message delivered to `info@pandacodegen.com` roughly 2 September — six days before
`39d5c3e` landed — and another around 7 September. Deliveries were therefore reaching the
inbox before the fix, so "both lead-capture paths returned 503 from the day it shipped until
8 September" is not supported by the evidence that actually matters. The failure was real
but its extent and duration are unestablished; it may have been intermittent, or specific to
one path or one condition.

What survives: a 503 path existed and was fixed on 8 September. What does not: any claim
about how many submissions were lost, or that the entire GSC window had no working enquiry
route. Do not use that period as a clean "forms were broken" explanation for the absence of
enquiries — the honest position is that enquiry capture was unreliable for an undetermined
part of it.

### Still to verify, in Vercel production environment variables

`src/app/api/submit-quote/route.ts:35` throws if `RESEND_API_KEY` is absent, and line 228
returns 503. `.env.local` has `RESEND_API_KEY` and `RESEND_FROM_EMAIL`, but that file is

**Check that `RESEND_API_KEY`, `RESEND_FROM_EMAIL` and `QUOTE_RECIPIENT_EMAIL` are set in

**What does not follow is the instruction to cut to a word count.** Being an outlier is

auditor withdrew the 1,200–1,600 target on 13 September as an unvalidated editorial

the length either.

**The surviving finding is duplication, and it is visible in the H2 list without interpretation:**

- "Founder-built, not farmed out" and "We are not a 'Digital Agency.'" — the same argument twice
- "Have a project or a question in mind?" (top) and "Need to move platforms without losing
  track of URLs, content, or integrations?" (bottom) — two enquiry blocks
- "Start with the situation" (eight situation cards) plus three service blocks — the same
  question asked twice
- "Which tier is right for me?" and "Make the commercial assumptions visible" — two
  pricing-adjacent blocks
- "What could faster be worth to you?" — the revenue calculator the audit recommended removing

De-duplication is a different operation from cutting: removing a repeated argument loses
nothing, and consolidating these lands the page near 12–13 sections — above median, no longer
an outlier — without deleting substance.

**Sequencing.** This is a structural change and must not share a measurement window with the
credibility corrections. Deploy 1: corrections only, then the 28-day lock with working forms.
Deploy 2: de-duplication, measured separately. Two changes, two windows, attributable.
