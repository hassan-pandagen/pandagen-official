# Service pages: hiring language and buying problems

9 October 2026. The owner asked to use the measured terms, research missing opportunities and improve the service pages so visitors looking to hire can identify the relevant offer. This is an applied copy/navigation pass, with preserved page addresses and the existing visual design.

## Decision and evidence

Use a plainly named service in the page title and main heading, then identify a concrete problem the work can address. Explain the scope, price basis, relevant project and next step. A broad “best platform” or “what is CMS” query can support a guide; it should not replace the hiring proposition on a service page.

The two [owner-supplied batches](evidence/keyword-batch-owner-2026-10-09.json), [batch two](evidence/keyword-batch-owner-2026-10-09-02.json) contain 21 unique seeds, 13 numerical estimates and eight no-data results. These are US provider estimates with differing update dates. The mapping below keeps those estimates separate from externally supported phrases without measured volume. No related-query totals are added together, and no organic ranking difficulty was measured.

A further 19 candidate lookups were attempted through the documented anonymous endpoint. All received HTTP 403 Cloudflare Error 1010, reporting that the request signature was denied. [The attempt record](evidence/service-keyword-direct-2026-10-09.json) contains errors, not volume measurements. No fresh estimates are asserted for those candidates. The local research helper now stops on access-denied or rate-limit responses; no client-signature or access-control workaround was used.

## External research behind the unmeasured phrases

[Wix's performance guidance](https://support.wix.com/en/article/why-does-my-wix-site-load-slowly) discusses mobile content and performance factors. It supports reviewing the current site instead of claiming every slow Wix website needs a migration. [Wix Website Designers' redesign service](https://www.wixwebsitedesigners.com/service/website-redesign) explicitly offers redesign work; this establishes a service category, not a US monthly volume or a ranking opportunity.

[Squarespace's template guidance](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205815598-Switching-templates-in-version-7-0) distinguishes version 7.0 template switching from the style/layout changes available in 7.1. The copy offers a redesign without implying a 7.1 template switch. [Endeavor Design Studio's published redesign offer](https://www.endeavordesignstudio.com/services/squarespace-website-redesign) describes improving an established Squarespace/Wix site; it is another category example, not evidence of ranking ease.

[Webflow's Greenhouse case](https://webflow.com/customers/greenhouse) describes CMS/editor autonomy and a staged migration with training. Its results are Webflow/customer claims, not evidence for PandaCodeGen. The relevant buying job is team publishing and implementation fit, rather than assuming Webflow is inherently the wrong platform. [Webflow's hiring directory](https://webflow.com/hire/cms-integration) also names redesign, CMS integrations and custom work.

[WooCommerce's Store API advisory](https://developer.woocommerce.com/2024/04/01/changes-to-store-api-loading-for-performance/) describes customer-facing cart and checkout functionality and a specific performance change. It supports inspecting the live purchase path; the advisory is not a current whole-store diagnosis or a reason to promise a fixed speed score. [Shopify's partner directory](https://www.shopify.com/partners/directory/partner/ecommerce-partners4) names store builds/redesigns, troubleshooting and integrations as commercial work.

[HighLevel's external-form instructions](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000004524) document forms on other website platforms. [HireGHL's integration service](https://hireghl.com/services/gohighlevel-integration) describes connecting business tools. Together they support investigating the website-to-CRM handoff. They do not establish compatibility with every customer account or prove PandaCodeGen has completed every named integration.

[Supabase for agencies](https://supabase.com/solutions/agencies) describes a managed Postgres backend for client applications. It does not replace application design, permissions or the project's maintenance responsibilities. [thoughtbot's services](https://thoughtbot.com/services) connect software delivery with product/workflow needs and scoped development. The backend and custom-software pages now name those concrete jobs before technical implementation details.

[WebFX's CMS service](https://www.webfx.com/web-development/services/cms/) uses a commercial CMS label alongside buying information. [Google's AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) defines AEO/GEO and relates generative AI search to SEO fundamentals. These support naming the offer plainly and explaining its scope, while avoiding citation, ranking or sales guarantees.

The purpose is to answer a visitor's buying question clearly. Additional FAQ markup is not presented as a rich-result growth tactic or a guarantee of AI inclusion. The offline assistant retrieval check found all 13 exact answers with the correct service URLs and no unresolved pins; no live AI model request was made.
