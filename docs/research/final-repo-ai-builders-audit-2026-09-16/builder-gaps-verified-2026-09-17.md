# What the builders hand back to you, from their own documentation

Researched 17 September 2026 to answer a direct question: how many of the 59 checklist items
does an AI website builder miss? **That number cannot be produced without building the same
brief in each product and preserving the prompts, outputs and tests.** No builder was tested
here. What follows is what each vendor's own documentation says it does not do, which is a
different and more defensible claim.

## Two claims that did not survive checking

Recording these because they are a live example of the problem this research is about.

A web search returned a confident summary stating that Lovable users had spent **"3 days
trying to understand why form submissions aren't saving"** because of a Supabase Row Level
Security misconfiguration, attributed to a DEV Community article. Fetching that article
found it mentions Lovable only in a list of tools, and says nothing about Supabase, Row
Level Security, or three days. The summary assembled it from somewhere else.

The same search reported a **"security scan of 1,400 publicly available AI-coded apps"**
finding over 2,000 high-impact vulnerabilities. A second search produced "close to 5,600
vibe-coded applications" with 2,000+ vulnerabilities and 400+ exposed secrets. Different
population, unverified in both cases.

Neither figure is used. A search summary is not a source.

## Replit SEO Agent — primary source

[Replit's own documentation](https://docs.replit.com/features/publishing/seo-agent),
retrieved 17 September 2026.

**What it checks:** crawlability and indexing via `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml` and link
structure; unique descriptive `<title>` tags and meta descriptions; JSON-LD structured data;
Open Graph and social tags; semantic markup covering heading order, landmark elements and
image `alt` text.

**What the documentation says it does not do**, verbatim:

> "SEO Agent makes sure the app you have built is technically sound for search, and does not
> write content or build topical authority for you."

**Conditions that matter commercially:** it requires a published app and a paid plan. It does
not run on unpublished apps or on free plans.

An independent 2026 review of Replit's SEO Agent also notes it does nothing for indexation
velocity — no IndexNow ping on deploy, no Google Indexing API, no automatic submission to
Search Console or Bing Webmaster Tools — and that a React frontend generated by the Agent is
likely to deploy as a client-side SPA whose initial HTML document is close to empty. That is
a third-party reading, not Replit's own wording, and is marked as such.
[Alva Cardona, June 2026](https://aecardonac.medium.com/replits-seo-agent-vs-92-replit-seo-moves-what-got-automated-and-what-still-needs-you-b1a46c4e59eb)

## The pattern across all of them

Every builder examined documents capable tooling and then hands the verification back:

| Builder | What its own documentation reserves for you |
| --- | --- |
| Replit | Content and topical authority. Requires a published app on a paid plan. |
| Lovable | SEO elements are not always generated up front, and reviews do not rerun automatically when you publish. Security scanning "does not replace a thorough review or guarantee complete security"; use-case requirements are assigned to the owner. |
| Bolt.new | Credentials and configuration, plus testing of successful payments, failed payments, webhooks and subscription flows. Hosting analytics include bots with no built-in way to separate them. |
| v0 | Adding authentication, functionality and optimisation iteratively. A described test capability is not evidence any specific workflow was exercised. |
| Webflow AI | Plan and credit limits, primary-locale restrictions, and component and CMS audit blind spots. "No issues found" is not verification of every route. |
| Wix Harmony | An accessibility wizard with documented coverage limits. |

## Why this is a recurring cost, not a launch task

The line that matters for a business owner is not in any capability list. It is
**"reviews do not rerun automatically when publishing"**, and its equivalents.

A generated site is checked once, at generation. The business then changes: a page is added,
a price is edited, a form field is renamed, a plan lapses, a payment provider rotates a key.
Nothing re-runs. Nobody is told. The checks in the three screenshot lists are all one-time
inspections of a page, and none of the 59 asks whether an enquiry still arrives this week.

## The form is the documented weak point, independently

[Abhilash Veettil, DEV Community, 21 June](https://dev.to/eabhvee/why-your-ai-generated-contact-form-is-not-working-4o3o)
lists five reasons an AI-generated contact form does not work: no real action URL, the wrong
HTTP method ("many AI-generated examples use GET by default. Contact forms should almost
always use POST"), missing name attributes, no spam protection, and **no submission
visibility**.

That last one is the same fault this site found in its own form, arrived at independently.
Our quote form returned a success screen and discarded the message for any submission
completed in under two seconds, and we only found it by reading the email delivery log
against a real submission.

## What this supports the article saying

- Builders handle roughly half the technical SEO list by documentation, on conditions.
- No builder writes the content or supplies the copywriting judgements.
- Every builder hands verification back to the owner, in its own words.
- None of it re-runs when the business changes, which is what makes this recurring work
  rather than a launch task.

It does not support a failure rate, a count of items missed, or any claim that a named
builder fails a check we have not run.
