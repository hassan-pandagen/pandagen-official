# Service pages: structure, copy, work and enquiry comparison

Reviewed 14 September 2026. This is the service-page portion of the deeper service and blog audit. It audits the current repository; it does not describe changes already implemented. No application code was changed for this report.

**The service pages need a clearer buying sequence, and several concrete inconsistencies need correction before a visual redesign.** The strongest material already exists: real projects, platform-specific migration details, editing boundaries, explicit starting prices, and source-linked reviews. It is repeatedly interrupted by platform criticism, publication methodology and restatements that the quote is what counts. The service index also demonstrates capabilities with fictional interfaces while the redesigned work page now has real project previews.

## Scope, method and confidence

All eight canonical service details, the service index and the WordPress alias were inspected for source structure, hero copy, commercial expectations, proof, CTA labels/destinations and shared components. [The source inventory](service-own-source-inventory.json) preserves file paths, line numbers, explicit section counts and heading/component order. Shared component internals were read separately. The alias permanently redirects to `/services/wordpress-migration`; it should remain an alias. [Redirect source](../../../src/app/services/wordpress/page.tsx#L4).

External primary pages were retrieved on 14 September 2026: seven relevant service/practice pages across Bejamas, Naturaily, FocusReactive and Fueled's 10up practice, plus two service hubs. Pagepro's service URLs were also requested; web extraction returned an iframe-only document. The main audit's browser attempts on its Next.js and WordPress services reached 403 security interstitials at both viewport sizes. No structure is inferred from those access failures. [Source and access register](service-source-notes.md).

The comparison below concerns observed content order and link language. It is not competitor conversion research, and no agency's revenue, superiority, performance claims or customer outcomes were independently verified. Source extraction may include hidden/responsive duplicates and is not a visual viewport measurement. Browser findings are recorded separately by the main audit. Prior July/August audits and the earlier 14 September report were leads, not substitutes for checking current source.

Priority definitions: **P1** = a concrete copy, evidence or destination inconsistency; **P2** = a proposed improvement to clarity and decision flow; **P3** = refinement after those changes. A P2 recommendation is a design hypothesis, not a measured conversion loss.

### Browser evidence supplied by the main audit

All nine own pages were rendered from the current local production build on port 3002 at 1440 × 1000 and 390 × 844. The checks found no horizontal overflow or page errors. Mobile service details spanned roughly 25,168–36,329 document pixels including navigation/footer; that is descriptive, not a target length. The significant issue is what the reader reaches along that distance. [Local runtime capture](../../../audit/service-blog-deep-2026-09-14/visual-audit.json).

Naturaily's Shopify document measured about 14,525 px desktop and 21,307 px mobile in the final capture. Logos and a named testimonial appeared before its detailed case collection. Final screenshots were captured after optional cookies were disabled through the preference controls. This benchmark does not establish that service pages must be short or start with complete cases. ## What the current comparison actually shows

### Bejamas: service hub

Its hub organizes the offer by four engagement phases, followed by work and buying questions. Each phase describes services and tangible outputs or what to begin with. That makes an audit understandable as purchased work rather than repeated reassurance that discovery is necessary. The introduction names an organizational problem: fragmented content systems and publishing dependency. It also carries a client-specific result link. Its positioning addresses larger multi-brand estates; Panda should not adopt that scope simply because it sounds established. Header enquiry language includes “Talk to us.” [Bejamas services](https://bejamas.com/services).

### Bejamas: website migration

Observed sequence: offer → section navigation and client logos → definition → included work → source platforms → delivery method → named testimonial → search-continuity controls and another testimonial → cost drivers → partner selection → company perspective → migration examples → FAQs. It explains which inventories and complexity factors determine the quote; it does not present a universal flat price. Case examples are relatively late, so this is not evidence that every established agency shows a giant screenshot immediately. The useful pattern is the connection between scope, delivery artifacts, named work and pricing logic. Some search-preservation language is stronger than Panda should reproduce without supporting records. [Bejamas website migration](https://bejamas.com/services/website-migration).

### Bejamas: custom development

Observed sequence: direct service heading → fit statement and section links → client logos → early contact path → definition and inclusions → custom/template decision table → specific reasons with project links → testimonial → work → commercial questions/FAQs. It separates the design responsibility from the engineering responsibility. “Talk to us” and “View all work” serve different readiness levels. Its template comparison is useful as a decision aid, but its categorical examples and broad AI/search benefits are not validated findings. Panda can use a concrete software task and existing project to explain custom development without adopting the enterprise positioning or claims. [Bejamas custom development](https://bejamas.com/services/custom-web-development).

### Naturaily: service hub

The introduction states the principal capability families and offers two immediate routes: “Get an estimate” and “See case studies.” Core service groups distinguish custom websites, headless systems, Shopify and broader commerce. Each pairs typical buyer outcomes with common work types. It then introduces technology breadth, attributed reviews, contact and FAQs. The FAQs clarify which service category fits a buyer and what the first conversation covers. Panda's equivalent choice should be equally explicit, while retaining its own smaller set of capabilities and avoiding unsupported general outcome promises. [Naturaily services](https://naturaily.com/services).

### Naturaily: headless CMS

Observed sequence: audience/offer and consultation CTA → benefits → service menu → delivery stages → named team → technologies → more benefits and estimate CTA → substantial project collection → reasons to choose the agency → reviews → closing call CTA → FAQs → related resources. The stages distinguish the agency's action from what the client receives. Project entries name industries and implementation contexts. This is a long page with repeated benefit arguments, not a model to copy wholesale. Relevant lessons are editorial workflow specificity, named people, and matching examples to the CMS capability. Its “GET A FREE CONSULTATION” and later “SCHEDULE A CALL” are explicit actions; the proposed buyer does not need to infer a deliverable from “planning path.” [Naturaily headless CMS](https://naturaily.com/services/headless-cms-agency).

### Naturaily: Shopify

Observed sequence: Shopify offer/estimate CTA → buyer situations → attributed testimonials → service families → contact CTA → migration scope → storefront-choice table → engagement types → delivery stages → reasons to choose → Shopify project collection → adjacent specialties → reviews → final CTA/FAQs. “GET A Shopify project estimate” names the actual next step. The storefront table distinguishes theme, custom theme, headless/Hydrogen and Plus; project examples distinguish storefront, operations and payment-integration work. Some traffic-preservation statements remain overly absolute. Panda should transfer the clarity of retained/rebuilt responsibilities and relevant work, not assume this longer page or its statistics converts better. [Naturaily Shopify development](https://naturaily.com/services/shopify-development-company).

### FocusReactive: Next.js

Observed sequence: service and geography → partner credential → expertise → build/modernization/support options → recent project facts → named engineering perspective → technical differences → audit/handover practices → technology set → project showcase → engagement types → FAQs → articles → enquiry form. Project cards specify industry, technology and the agency's role. The close states a response, call and proposal sequence, then requests budget/timeframe and project information. This makes responsibilities and next steps inspectable. Some technical copy is repetitive, and the extracted form reports a security-verification loading issue; extraction alone cannot establish real-user form failure. Panda should use the role/context pattern with its actual evidence. [FocusReactive Next.js development](https://focusreactive.com/next-js-expert-agency/).

### FocusReactive: CMS migration

Observed sequence: migration offer → partner logos → business problem → extensive platform directory → architecture comparison → differentiators → recent migrations → operating approach → FAQs → enquiry form. Work entries show industry, stack, scope and live links. The FAQ acknowledges that a traditional CMS can suit a simple site. The very large platform directory and repeated broad superiority claims make this a mixed benchmark. Panda does not need a platform-by-platform permutation matrix or new service URLs to improve the eight pages it already has. [FocusReactive CMS migration](https://focusreactive.com/cms-migration-services/).

### Fueled: 10up WordPress practice

This is an enterprise WordPress practice, so it is an adjacent delivery/presentation comparison rather than the same offer or budget. The service is demonstrated through named projects immediately after the introduction, then concrete products/integrations, publisher and commerce applications, support models, partnerships, open-source contributions and tools. The recurring project action is “Read Case Study.” It is especially useful for showing what the team actually contributed and for recognizing that well-run WordPress is a viable service outcome. Its prestige, partnership and impact claims are agency-published, not independently audited here. Panda should not imitate its scale or claim all WordPress owners need to leave. [Fueled 10up practice](https://fueled.com/wordpress/).

### Headings, summaries and message hierarchy

Current service H1s often sound like blog introductions. WordPress promises an explanation of costs and rebuild fit; WooCommerce promises an explanation of checkout slowness. Wix, Squarespace and custom engineering append process qualification to an already long problem statement. The resulting page initially sounds like advice, then becomes an agency offer. This is a content-role observation, not evidence that these headings cannot rank. [WordPress H1](../../../src/app/services/wordpress-migration/PageContent.tsx#L49), [WooCommerce H1](../../../src/app/services/woocommerce/PageContent.tsx#L57), [engineering H1](../../../src/app/services/custom-engineering/PageContent.tsx#L51).

Use a service H1 that identifies the work, with the buyer's situation in the supporting sentence. A conditional problem statement is preferable to asserting an unmeasured defect: “When your checkout is holding the store back” is more defensible than telling every reader their checkout is their slowest page. The service can then state how the team investigates and delivers.

The answer-first summaries are potentially useful, but they currently compress many separate commitments into one block: technology, ownership, price, delivery, redirects, targets, remedies and provider costs. The WordPress summary is a notable example. It also repeats information immediately above and below. [WordPress summary](../../../src/app/services/wordpress-migration/PageContent.tsx#L88), [commerce summary](../../../src/app/services/ecommerce/PageContent.tsx#L80), [engineering summary](../../../src/app/services/custom-engineering/PageContent.tsx#L81).

A better service introduction needs one clear offer sentence, one fit/constraint sentence and one short commercial line. A separate TL;DR box is optional. Structured, extractable prose should still be natural for a human buyer; adding a `data-speakable` attribute does not establish that the message is useful or that an answer engine will select it. The blog-specific summary recommendations are handled in the article audit.

### CTA wording and destination contract

A repeated button is acceptable when the buyer reaches a new decision point. The problem is that apparently equivalent labels currently trigger different experiences: a Cal booking button versus an `open-quote-modal` event. A promise of a plan may lead only to an enquiry step. Label the immediate action, and explain the outcome separately. These destination observations are from source; successful booking/submission requires runtime verification. [Hero booking](../../../src/app/services/wix/PageContent.tsx#L58), [tier enquiry event](../../../src/components/services/PricingTiers.tsx#L109).

| Placement | Proposed label | Actual destination and accompanying explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Service hero, migration | Book a migration call | Existing booking flow. Short note: bring the URL and the change you need; scope and final price follow review. Use only the response/call commitments the business actually supports. |
| Service hero, engineering | Book an engineering call | Existing booking flow; the call establishes fit and information needed, not a complete specification. |
| Service hero, GHL | Book a GHL integration call | Existing booking flow; make retaining the CRM explicit. |
| Secondary hero link | See a related project / See how it works | Direct case link or a functioning in-page anchor. An evidence link should reach the evidence, not an external homepage without context. |
| Pricing card | Request a quote | Existing quote form/modal. Carry the selected service/tier if supported; do not silently pretend this is already implemented. |
| Close after FAQ | Book a migration call / Book an engineering call | Same existing booking action as the corresponding hero. Keep enquiry-form actions separately labeled. |
| Evidence note | How we publish results | `/editorial-policy#publication-controls`, following the work-page fix. |

The proposed starting layout has **three potential decision points**: first offer, after meaningful scope/proof, and after objections. This is not an empirically established optimum. A price selection can have its own action. Review the global footer CTA alongside the page close so it does not create two consecutive generic pitches. Keep phone/email alternatives accessible without making every paragraph another action.

## Implementation sequence and review criteria

1. **Correct contradictions first:** proof sources, decorative numbers, service-specific labels, quote expectations, pricing units, unqualified platform assumptions and cross-platform case implications. Keep a small change record so structural work does not conceal factual corrections.
2. **Create the shared project presentation:** real image, role, client relationship, scope, direct case link and optional supported result. Use existing project facts rather than a second roster. A relevant related project is acceptable when honestly labeled.
3. **Refactor one commercial template:** begin with WordPress migration, then engineering. They cover two distinct buying journeys. Consolidate `PartnerPromise` and pricing qualifications without deleting terms or evidence methods buyers need.
4. **Adapt, do not duplicate:** apply the visual foundation to each platform while retaining its migration boundaries and appropriate proof. Shopify and GHL need retained-system diagrams; builder migrations need content/functionality maps; engineering needs tasks and delivery artifacts.
5. **Check the full journey:** desktop/mobile, heading order, real screenshot readability, anchors clearing the header, keyboard/focus, CTA destination, service/tier carried into the form where implemented, error state, and next-step copy. Do not send test enquiries without authorization.
6. **Evaluate behavior after release:** record service → case, service → enquiry, enquiry-start and completed-enquiry events with service and placement. Compare comparable periods and traffic sources; small lead counts require caution. No predicted conversion uplift is justified by this research alone.

Success is a buyer being able to state, after scanning: what Panda builds, whether the service fits, what relevant work exists, what is included or scoped separately, what determines cost/timing, who owns and maintains the result, and exactly what happens after clicking the main CTA. The page does not need to restate the publication policy to answer those questions.
