# Blog structure for a specialist web development agency

The strongest reference for PandaCodeGen is a small set of article formats with different jobs: a buyer guide that helps someone make a decision, an implementation guide that resolves a technical problem, and a project account that shows what the team actually did. The researched agencies do not use one universal section sequence. Their useful common ground is visible authorship, descriptive headings, a clear route through the article, and a next step connected to its subject.

This comparison covers eleven complete articles on seven publishers' own websites, accessed on **14 September 2026**. Pagepro, FocusReactive, Naturaily and Bejamas are the closest headless development and migration references. Prismic and Sanity are adjacent software vendors, whose product adoption goals differ from an agency's lead qualification goals. 10up supplies an enterprise WordPress engineering contrast. The sample was chosen for relevance and format variety; it is not a ranking of market share, organic performance or conversion rates.

## What the comparison supports

**Help the reader make progress before asking them to buy.** Pagepro's migration guide starts its substantive body with a summary and a stay-or-migrate decision. Naturaily's agency selection guide gives the fit decision in the opening description. FocusReactive's broader migration guide starts with takeaways that include cost and scope. These are observed editorial choices, not proof of commercial effectiveness. [Pagepro migration guide](https://pagepro.co/blog/wordpress-to-sanity-migration-guide/), [Naturaily agency guide](https://naturaily.com/blog/headless-cms-development-services), [FocusReactive migration guide](https://focusreactive.com/blog/cms-migration/).

**Proof is most useful when it explains a decision.** Bejamas places billing and performance screenshots beside its account of hosting and rendering decisions. Prismic's blog rebuild account includes design requests, old/new screens and implementation examples. These make the stories inspectable, while leaving open whether the reported business changes came from any individual design feature. [Bejamas rebuild](https://bejamas.com/insights/migration/how-we-rebuilt-our-website-in-2024), [Prismic rebuild](https://prismic.io/blog/how-we-built-the-prismic-blog).

**A technical article can be narrow and still demonstrate expertise.** Prismic's content-model tutorial organizes its code around specific changes a developer needs to make. 10up's short plugin announcement explains one migration problem and the tool built to address it. Neither requires a long commercial introduction. [Prismic model changes](https://prismic.io/blog/changing-content-models-on-a-live-site), [10up plugin announcement](https://10up.com/blog/2021/convert-to-blocks/).

The recommendation is therefore to redesign the reading system and article briefs together. A more attractive wrapper cannot fix an article that delays its answer, mixes buyer and developer intent, or substitutes claims for examples.

## Comparison by design decision

| Decision | Evidence from the sample | Recommended rule for PandaCodeGen |
|---|---|---|
| Opening summary | Explicit in the two Pagepro guides, FocusReactive migration and Naturaily agency selection; other formats work without one | Use a short authored answer for substantial decision guides. Do not make a summary mandatory for a brief build note |
| Table of contents | Present in Pagepro, FocusReactive, Prismic and the Sanity utility introduction | Add it when it helps navigate distinct tasks. Keep mobile controls compact and keyboard accessible |
| Heading intent | Buyer questions, technical tasks and narrative stages all appear | Choose headings from the reader's job. Do not force every heading into a question or keyword variant |
| Evidence | Named cases, working artifacts, diagrams, screenshots, commands and hypothetical examples serve different roles | Label the evidence type. Put the useful artifact beside the claim or instruction it supports |
| Authorship | Every sampled article names an author; several identify a relevant role | Link to the real author. Add technical review only when someone actually performed it |
| Freshness | Dates are visible, but separate updated dates are uncommon in this sample | Separate published and materially reviewed dates; include version scope for instructions |
| Commercial next step | Consultations in buyer guides; docs/tools in tutorials; end invitations in stories | Match the next action to the article. A qualified service link is usually more useful than repeated generic booking blocks |

These are descriptive observations across a deliberately small sample. There is no evidence here that a contents list, summary, CTA position or particular article length causes better rankings or conversion.

## Three article blueprints to implement

### A. Buyer decision or comparison

The reader is deciding whether to migrate, which approach fits, what it will involve, or whether to engage a specialist. The opening should answer the actual question in plain language and state the key condition. For example, an article about leaving a visual builder should explain whether its current limitation can be fixed in place before explaining a custom rebuild.

Recommended sequence:

1. A specific title, short answer and real author/date.
2. A compact set of outcomes or decisions the reader can reach, with jump links where useful.
3. A situation-based comparison: stay, improve the current implementation, or migrate.
4. A relevant example showing the source problem, selected scope and what had to be rebuilt.
5. Cost and time drivers using the agency's documented scope, with assumptions beside any estimate.
6. Risks, dependencies and who maintains the result.
7. A concise decision checklist and one relevant service route.

An FAQ is optional. It earns space only when it answers genuine remaining questions. It should not repeat the summary, comparison and conclusion in slightly different words.

### B. Technical diagnosis or implementation

The reader is trying to accomplish one operation. Begin with the observed symptom or intended result and the environment in which the guide applies. Code should be readable text, include only necessary context, and distinguish working implementation from pseudocode.

Recommended sequence:

1. Symptom or task, affected versions, and a brief resolution outline.
2. Prerequisites and a way to confirm the problem.
3. The smallest useful example, with expected output or a screenshot.
4. Explanation of why the change works and where it does not apply.
5. Verification and any relevant recovery step.
6. Primary documentation and a related technical article.
7. Optional service assistance for the reader whose situation exceeds the example.

Keep sales copy outside the code sequence. For a migration script, a mapped record and a validation result can demonstrate more competence than several paragraphs about being expert developers.

### C. Project or engineering account

The reader is evaluating judgment and delivery capability. The story should identify the relationship, starting situation and constraints before claiming an outcome. Screenshots can show the interface, while a diagram can explain how data, content or orders move through the system.

Recommended sequence:

1. Project context, relationship and the completed change.
2. Starting problem and constraints, with a real baseline only when available.
3. The consequential decision and alternatives considered.
4. Screenshots or a workflow diagram with explanatory captions.
5. What was difficult, changed during delivery, or remains limited.
6. Results with dates, definitions and comparable measurement conditions where supported.
7. A concise lesson, case-study link and relevant service path.

The CRM portfolio can support an engineering account of a specific workflow. A short demonstration of permissions, order status or data synchronization is more informative than a generic tour of an enterprise dashboard. Client data must remain protected and the founder relationship must stay explicit where applicable.

## A practical reading system for PandaCodeGen

Implement a shared visual foundation with format-specific modules, rather than a different bespoke page for every post. The foundation should cover readable type and line length, consistent heading spacing, links that are clearly distinguishable, responsive media, code overflow, and visible keyboard focus. These are proposed design requirements; the competitor comparison is not a substitute for testing them on PandaCodeGen's own pages.

The article header needs a descriptive title, a short standfirst, category, author and honest date. A large decorative image should not push the answer down unnecessarily. Use a project screenshot, annotated technical image or small diagram when it explains the subject. A title repeated inside a banner adds little information.

The body needs a small collection of reliable components: prose, semantic comparison table, numbered procedure, code block, figure/caption, concise evidence note and contextual service link. Build one accessible mobile behavior for each. A contents rail should never squeeze the reading column, and a mobile contents control should not obscure the article or primary navigation.

The end of the article should resolve the reader's next decision. Offer a closely relevant guide for further learning, a project example for proof and the appropriate service if assistance is warranted. These can be a compact set of links, rather than another full landing page beneath every post.

For a small expert agency, publication capacity is part of the design. Avoid modules that require constant moderation, invented popularity signals or a large original research budget. A few detailed examples the team can maintain are more credible than a universal template filled with statistics, enterprise badges and nominally current dates.

## What not to copy

- **Aggressive certainty.** A competitor's statement about guaranteed traffic preservation, security, AI visibility or delivery duration is not evidence that PandaCodeGen can promise it.
- **A year changed without a review.** Preserve durable URLs. Update the visible date only when the content has actually been materially checked or revised.
- **Every enterprise qualifier.** Large multinational publishing estates have different needs from a small business storefront or a founder-operated service company.
- **Every vendor conversion device.** A CMS vendor benefits from developer signups, community activity and product adoption. PandaCodeGen primarily needs the right buyer to understand its services and proof.
- **Decorative proof.** A generic dashboard screenshot should not appear to substantiate a project result. Captions should say what the image shows and whose environment it is.
- **Borrowed figures.** Even when a competitor provides an attractive before/after number, the comparison cannot establish an equivalent outcome on another site.

## Evidence limitations and source register

All eleven sources above were inspected through their complete accessible article bodies, including closing CTAs and navigation modules returned with the article. The report assesses content structure and observable publishing elements. It does not claim that text extraction establishes viewport placement, typography, animation, sticky behavior or accessibility. Those questions belong to the separate browser audit.

Publication dates are the labels displayed by the publishers, not independently verified revision histories. The older Bejamas, Prismic, Sanity and 10up examples remain useful format references, while their product instructions, prices and platform claims may be historical. The comparison does not validate every technical claim in these articles.

Human Made's [blog](https://humanmade.com/blog/) returned HTTP 403 during access. It is excluded from the article count and no page-structure finding is inferred from that failure.
