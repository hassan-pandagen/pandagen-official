# UK and Canada: market and language recommendation

Research checked 15 September 2026. Scope: public primary sources and agency service pages; no application changes. This is a market-entry recommendation, not a measured keyword-volume ranking. No GSC queries were used to infer external demand. No new volume, CPC or keyword-difficulty export was available in this research pass.

## Recommendation

Target the UK and English-speaking Canada with the existing English offer first. The commercial fit is websites and small online stores from **US$1,500**, custom work quoted to scope, direct founder contact, an editor and training, and client ownership. Keep AI SEO as a distinct monthly offer from **US$500**, with an explicit recurring scope.

This is the lowest operational-friction expansion: it reuses the language of sales conversations, project discovery, documentation and training. It does not imply easy rankings or that either country has more unserved demand than Germany or France. A country-qualified Google result is often competing with agencies that have local projects, reviews and relationships; changing a country name cannot reproduce those advantages.

Use a focused acquisition test in each country. Do not produce a cloned `/uk/`, `/ca/`, and city-page network simply because both countries use English. A useful country page becomes reasonable when it can answer actual differences: quote currency, payment arrangements, working-hour overlap, local examples, delivery process and buyer concerns. No invented local office or local-founder implication.

UK copy can naturally use "bespoke", "enquiries" and "optimisation"; Canadian copy can use "custom", "inquiries" and "optimization". These are editorial choices, not reasons by themselves to create duplicate English routes. Both markets understand the main service vocabulary.

For a later Quebec test, native-review seeds should include **conception de site web**, **création de site web**, **refonte de site web**, **site web pour PME**, **création de boutique en ligne**, **développement web sur mesure**, **référencement pour les moteurs de recherche IA**, **agence GEO**, and **optimisation pour les moteurs génératifs**. The AI terms are particularly unsettled; validate what appears in real local results instead of treating a literal translation as established demand.

Do not create several money pages for near-synonyms such as "AI SEO", "AEO" and "GEO" with the same offer. Test the terminology, then choose one service destination and useful supporting informational pages.

## Canada: language and currency decisions

1. Start with an English journey for Canada. Ontario, Alberta and British Columbia are reasonable initial acquisition segments to investigate, but this report does not establish a province ranking by demand or conversion likelihood.
2. Display **US$1,500** and **US$500/month** if the contract and invoice remain in USD. A bare dollar sign is ambiguous to Canadian buyers. Do not label the same number CAD or invent a fixed conversion without a pricing decision.
3. Do not treat the existing `/fr` translation as automatic Quebec localisation. French-Canadian vocabulary, prices, examples, contact options, confirmations, proposals and actual support capacity must all be coherent. A website written in French followed by an English-only sales conversation is a service constraint to disclose and resolve.
4. Quebec has specific French-language business requirements. The [OQLF business guidance](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/francisation/entreprises/) is the primary reference. Applicability to this business's particular cross-border selling model needs separate review; this report does not conclude that every overseas agency must publish an entire French site, or that a translation alone establishes compliance.

## Implementation principles for both markets

- Keep the truthful broad offer: new websites, online stores, redesigns and migrations; software where it is a real service. Country context belongs in useful delivery information and campaigns, not as a replacement for what is sold.
- State the working relationship and scheduling availability. Use real founder photographs, exact company details, named work and clear ownership disclosure. Local competitors may have local proof; transparent remote delivery is a better claim than implying a presence the company lacks.
- Use one English URL when it represents the same offer. Google supports regional versions and `hreflang`, but country copies are not a prerequisite for cross-border sales. If real regional variants are introduced, configure language/region annotations and canonical treatment intentionally, and let users select another version. See [Google's multilingual and multi-regional guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites).
- Do not broaden every article into a general company advert. Keep its problem-specific title, then make the relevant commercial invitation and current company facts accurate.
- Segment enquiries, qualified opportunities and won projects by country and service. This is the practical evidence that should determine whether to invest further in UK, Canada or a translated European funnel.

## Evidence needed before claiming a keyword opportunity

Obtain separate UK-English and Canada-English keyword exports, plus Canada-French for Quebec only if the French sales journey can be staffed. Use a named 12-month period and monthly breakdown; record collection date, provider, country/location, language, network, currency and close-variant handling. Preserve raw outputs. Competitor-page terms and business statistics cannot substitute for those exports, and the prior US-only DataForSEO capture cannot be relabelled as UK or Canadian demand.

For each candidate family, inspect a country-specific results sample and label: commercial agency, DIY tool, job, educational, or local intent. Assign one primary destination per buying intent. Use the resulting evidence to choose a small number of pages and acquisition tests, rather than expanding the whole site into multiple regional copies at once.
