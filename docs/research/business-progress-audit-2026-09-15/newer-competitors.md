# Boutique and specialist competitor benchmark

Research date: 15 September 2026. Scope: six company websites, selected service pages, portfolio/case examples and articles. This is a benchmark of publicly presented offers and evidence, not independent verification of their client results, market share, rankings or delivery quality. No forms were submitted and no sales conversations were undertaken. Prices describe each agency's own scope; they are not equivalent alternatives to PandaCodeGen's US$1,500 website entry project or US$500 monthly AI SEO engagement.

## Offer, proof and buyer journey

### 1. Bejamas

**Buyer and organisation.** The current homepage sells migration and continuing operation of complex websites, with editorial independence as the business benefit. Services are organised into four stages: audit, design, migrate and run. Each stage names outputs, such as an estate inventory, risk register, cost model, component library or continuing support. No numeric agency entry fee was found on the inspected pages. The main invitation is to talk to the team. [Homepage](https://bejamas.com/), [services](https://bejamas.com/services).

**Proof.** Work cards identify project, technology and business change, with filters by service, technology and industry. The Sitecore service ties its Bennetts example to three actual sites and makes the reader's potential saving dependent on their own baseline. Published percentages remain agency-reported outcomes; this research did not inspect the client's financial records. [Work](https://bejamas.com/work), [Sitecore service](https://bejamas.com/services/sitecore-migration).

**Lesson for PandaCodeGen.** Show the buyer what is delivered and what they can do afterwards. A content editor and training become more persuasive when illustrated by an actual publishing task. Migration terminology is appropriate on a migration service; broadening PandaCodeGen does not require deleting that valid intent.

### 2. Blazity

**Buyer and organisation.** Enterprise AI and engineering positioning, with five clear routes: AI engineering, performance, migration/modernisation, application development and technical consulting. Each route is attached to a recognisable operational problem. The CTA is "Talk to an architect"; the closing invitation accepts a problem before the prospect has a full technical brief. No numeric project floor was located on inspected pages. [Homepage](https://www.blazity.com/), [services](https://www.blazity.com/services).

**Proof.** CookUnity's case explains the revenue-critical subscription funnel, previous stack, incremental implementation, three-month engagement, internal engineering collaboration and continuing monitoring. It reports a 70% LCP improvement and sustained conversion gains. No raw measurement export or controlled attribution experiment was examined, so these remain reported results. Open-source solutions provide another type of inspectable engineering evidence, distinct from claims about business impact. [CookUnity](https://www.blazity.com/case-study/cookunity), [solutions](https://www.blazity.com/solutions).

**Lesson for PandaCodeGen.** Broader capability can stay comprehensible through separate buyer routes. Custom software needs examples of the workflow, user roles, integrations and delivered system. Listing frameworks establishes capability, but does not explain the engagement a buyer can purchase.

### 3. FocusReactive

**Buyer and organisation.** Content-heavy businesses and teams managing editors, markets and ecommerce. The homepage names headless CMS, replatforming, composable commerce, design/build, Next.js and advanced integrations. Projects appear early with industry, stack and scope labels. The closing form states response expectations, then asks for project subject, budget and timeframe. No numeric first-party entry price was found in this inspection. [Homepage](https://focusreactive.com/).

**Proof and limits.** Named client testimony, including QuantHealth, discusses performance, manual work and publishing efficiency. These are published testimonials, not independently audited measurements. The claimed percentage of projects meeting budget lacks a visible cohort definition in the inspected homepage text. AI SEO is embedded in technical delivery; the phrase alone does not establish a recurring monthly service.

**Lesson for PandaCodeGen.** Give each project a compact buyer-readable label: what business it serves, what was built and who uses it. The website editor is a practical operational benefit shared by strong competitors, not a unique claim that PandaCodeGen should present as exclusive.

### 4. Flowout

**Buyer and organisation.** Webflow services for B2B/SaaS and enterprise marketing teams. Its homepage pairs a call invitation with pricing and foregrounds client work. Pricing separates development, design, copywriting and additional ongoing disciplines. On the inspected pricing page, development is US$5,900/month on monthly billing; development plus design is US$7,900/month. These are recurring team-service packages, not one-off small-site build prices. The FAQ explains that unlimited requests enter a queue and are delivered sequentially. [Homepage](https://www.flowout.com/), [pricing](https://www.flowout.com/pricing).

**Proof and limits.** The ShipHero case gives the buyer's situation, scope, implementation and reported results. It lists form conversions from 576 to 872 and SQLs from 172 to 356, but the work index labels the latter as a 263% increase. Those SQL endpoints imply roughly 107%, not 263%; the page does not establish that both surfaces refer to different windows. Publication is not validation. Funding and valuation badges also describe clients, not revenue caused by agency work. [ShipHero](https://www.flowout.com/case-studies/shiphero-case-study), [work index](https://www.flowout.com/case-studies).

**Lesson for PandaCodeGen.** Copy the scope clarity and project presentation, not every numerical badge. Put the expected monthly workload and exclusions beside a retainer price. Show photographs/screenshots and meaningful scope without needing an unsupported uplift claim on every card.

### 5. Omnius

**Buyer and organisation.** Its current identity is organic growth for SaaS, fintech and AI businesses. It separates SEO/GEO, content, programmatic SEO, development/CRO and analytics. Its own memo explicitly limits the service focus instead of offering all marketing disciplines. The homepage offers contact and a meeting. No numeric retainer floor was found on the inspected homepage, GEO or analytics pages. [Homepage](https://www.omnius.so/), [memo](https://www.omnius.so/memo).

**Proof and limits.** The agency's own BOFU case defines conversions as booked meetings and completed forms, identifies its own analytics product and discusses missing attribution. That is better than an undefined lead count, but it does not mean 1,000 paying clients or 1,000 AI-attributed conversions. No raw export was audited. The GEO page also contains broad "citation engineering" and keyword-placement assertions that this benchmark does not establish as reliable search mechanisms. [Own-site case](https://www.omnius.so/blog/omnius-bofu-seo-strategy), [GEO service](https://www.omnius.so/geo-agency).

**Attribution offer.** A separate analytics page distinguishes tracking implementation, analysis, reporting and custom attribution. That helps a buyer recognise measurement as paid work in its own right. Its presence does not prove a fixed deliverable count or guarantee complete source recovery. [Analytics service](https://www.omnius.so/data-tracking-analytics).

**Lesson for PandaCodeGen.** Define each metric and each service. AI citations, assistant referrals, self-declared sources, enquiries and paid orders should not become one interchangeable success number. AEO and lead attribution can support each other while remaining separately scoped work.

### 6. MeasureMinds

**Buyer and organisation.** A specialist analytics agency targeting difficult enterprise measurement problems. Its homepage makes the discipline immediately clear and offers a consultation/audit. The GTM service specifies workshop, audit, account clean-up, tag mapping, server-side setup, training and ongoing support. This is a recognisable delivery sequence. No numeric consulting fee was found on the inspected pages. [Homepage](https://measuremindsgroup.com/), [GTM service](https://measuremindsgroup.com/google-tag-manager-agency).

**Proof and limits.** The GTM page links named client cases and offers free utilities as implementation evidence. Some testimonials refer to work before the company launch; these must not be counted as the age of the current agency. Its CAPI article is explicitly a webinar write-up with Publicis Sapient examples. Those examples are not MeasureMinds' own results. [CAPI article](https://measuremindsgroup.com/integrating-gtm-server-side-conversions-api).

**Lesson for PandaCodeGen.** A published CAPI tutorial demonstrates understanding; a client-facing service must also explain supported systems, access needs, event scope, testing, handover and maintenance. Use the specialist's clear implementation stages. Do not inherit claims from its article that hashing automatically makes data safe, that privacy controls need no further consideration, or that CAPI automatically prevents duplicate events.

## What a saleable tracking offer and its receipts should show

Recommendation for PandaCodeGen, not an assertion that the following artifacts already exist:

1. **Defined engagement:** which website/CRM/commerce stack, which events and destinations, existing integration versus custom work, implementation responsibility, hosting/vendor costs and support period.
2. **Implementation evidence:** redacted event map; test-journey record; event identity and duplicate-handling checks; source fields carried into the lead/order record; rejected/failed-event monitoring; and handover documentation.
3. **Measurement evidence:** dates, eligible event count, known missing sources, metric definitions, the system of record and a reconciliation method. More recorded events is measurement recovery, not automatically more actual sales.
4. **Business-outcome evidence:** if claiming improved ROAS, revenue or conversion rate, state the comparison window and confounding changes. Implementation screenshots alone do not prove those effects.
5. **Attribution boundaries:** AI referral capture and customer-declared discovery answer a different question from a server event sent to Meta. Keep those instruments labelled separately.

Google's server-side documentation explicitly carries user choices into server tag behaviour and describes redaction and control as configured capabilities. That supports treating privacy/consent behaviour as an implementation and verification requirement, not a benefit that appears automatically when code moves to a server. This source concerns Google's tag platform, not a substitute for Meta-specific specifications. [Consent implementation](https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/tag-manager/server-side/consent-mode?hl=en), [server-side control](https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/learn/sst-fundamentals/3-why-and-when-sst). Meta's detailed developer guidance could not be opened in this tool session; no current Meta API parameter or legal specification has been certified by this competitor pass.

## Strategic synthesis

The useful pattern is broad capability with clear routes, credible examples and an understandable engagement. PandaCodeGen can welcome buyers needing their first website while still serving migrations, software, measurement and AI search. That does not require one hero to name every technology or suggest every small build includes all five disciplines.

Recommended buyer routes are: new website/online store; improve or replace an existing site; custom software/integrations; measurement and attribution; and ongoing AI search work. Each route should lead to its own deliverables, price logic and relevant proof. A company's receipts need to be evaluated per claim; having an evidence policy and one first-party success does not establish every outcome across every service.

The benchmark does not establish that PandaCodeGen already outperforms these companies, that broader copy will rank, or that cheaper entry pricing yields a comparable service. Those conclusions require PandaCodeGen's deployed pages, actual qualified enquiries, wins and margins, plus country-specific demand evidence.
