## Details that prevent misleading comparisons

**A numerical allowance is not automatically a monthly allowance.** Preserve a vendor's stated cadence. Likewise, a tracked question, generated answer, website page, content asset and implemented task are different units. Do not convert between them without a definition.

**WebFX's price and quantities are in the same matrix.** Empty extracted cells were not treated as inclusions or exclusions. General service prose cannot resolve every package-specific gap.

**Visionary's linked SEO package is relevant because its GEO page explicitly connects them.** This is not an assumption that every SEO agency automatically includes GEO. Its quantities are published promises; this research does not establish the economics of delivering the claimed hours, content and links at that fee. Neither its stated price cap nor its acquisition method should become a PandaCodeGen promise.

**The other three are useful for work categories, not a fabricated price-per-output comparison.** An unpriced service description cannot establish a $1,500 entry rate. A strategy program's monthly billing does not mean every named initial artifact is produced again each month. An audit-first sequence does not by itself prove a separate setup invoice.

## What to borrow when defining PandaCodeGen's scope

The following is interpretation for offer design, not additional competitor fact or an approved customer commitment.

1. **Define the recurring responsibility.** A monthly engagement should lead from observation to a prioritised decision, an agreed improvement and a record of completed work. A dashboard alone does not explain what a customer is paying the agency to do.
2. **Separate onboarding from maintenance.** Access setup, baseline capture, measurement configuration and an initial backlog can consume most of a first month. Decide whether they replace normal monthly production, fit within it, or require a separately quoted initial phase. State the actual decision.
3. **Make the work unit understandable.** If using page improvements, explain the boundaries: one section edit and a new product template have very different effort. If using flexible work capacity, tell the customer what is agreed before the month and what happens to larger requests. Do not hide the scope behind a vague asset count.
4. **Separate monitoring coverage from implementation coverage.** A broad technical scan can be inexpensive to run while reviewing, prioritising and repairing the findings is substantial work. The quote should define both.
5. **Choose a meaningful measurement cadence.** Record the same question set, markets, languages and engines consistently where sampled tracking is in scope. Report actual changes and attributable enquiries separately from sampled appearances. More prompt runs are not automatically more customer value.
6. **Name the handoff.** Does PandaCodeGen ship the edit, supply a developer ticket, or collaborate with the customer's existing agency? Who supplies facts and approves content? The proposal should answer this without implying all platforms or all code changes are included.
7. **Define commercial boundaries with equal care.** Tool costs, content creation, outreach, extensive rebuilds, new integrations, revisions and urgent work need an explicit treatment. Unknown competitor terms are not a reason to omit PandaCodeGen's own terms.

The useful public promise is a **starting monthly engagement with a defined minimum scope and a written project-specific quote**. Supported domains, sections or brands may affect effort, but the user's instruction is not to turn that into a per-website tariff. Portfolio-wide work should be scoped as the actual engagement. Larger scope should change the quote before work is promised.
