# AI SEO retainer: proposed delivery scope and capacity

Prepared 15 September 2026. This is an offer-design recommendation, not an existing package, approved commitment, contract or statement of PandaCodeGen's actual costs. The owner has established a **$500/month starting point with a separate quote based on the project and scope**. Monthly delivery capacity, loaded labour cost and tooling cost have not been supplied. The examples below make the choices reviewable without pretending those facts are known.

Inputs: [current page draft](../ai-seo-page-for-review-2026-09-14.md), [buyer/implementation audit](../ai-seo-audit-2026-09-14/buyer-and-implementation.md), [14 September competitor benchmark](../ai-seo-audit-2026-09-14/competitor-comparison.md), and [current pricing terms](../../../src/app/pricing/PricingPageClient.tsx), particularly the optional-support explanation at line 388. Competitor observations are inherited from that dated research; this contribution does not claim a new price survey or independently verified competitor results.

## Recommendation

Use **a narrow, implementation-led engagement that combines buyer research, content improvement, technical checks and reporting** as the representative $500 starting scope. Reserve roughly **4–6 total working hours per client per month** for that example, including communication, QA and reporting. Confirm that this works with actual delivery costs before publishing it.

Do not sell recurring HTML/schema checks alone as a complete AI SEO programme. A site can already render correctly and still fail to explain why a relevant buyer should choose the business. The paid work should investigate those buyer questions and improve actual source pages, using technical implementation as an advantage.

The proposed minimum must remain a **scope example, not a price for every five-page website**. A fifty-page site with five agreed priority pages can be a smaller engagement than a five-page regulated site needing extensive expert review. Site size affects scope; it is not the whole pricing model.

The previous competitor research supports borrowing the operating pattern, not promising equivalent breadth: scope beside price, research translated into content, implementation responsibility, measurement definitions, a report the buyer can inspect, and visible engagement boundaries. Broad content/PR/authority programmes in that benchmark are not what this $500 example includes.

## A representative $500 monthly engagement

### Eligibility and coverage assumptions

- One business/brand, one principal service or product category, one language and one target market.
- Up to **five named priority URLs** on one existing, editable website. These are the URLs reviewed for the agreed objective, not an artificial maximum size of the customer's site.
- One CMS/site implementation environment; client supplies working access, a normal publishing path and existing analytics/Search Console access where available.
- Up to **three agreed business competitors**, reviewed for a specific question or page gap rather than a complete monthly competitor audit.
- **Ten agreed buyer questions across two named AI/search surfaces**, with monthly human analysis. If a licensed tool collects them more frequently, retain that history and report its actual schedule; do not imply daily human review. Without such a tool, the manual fallback is once per month: **20 recorded observations**, explicitly labelled a small sample. Select the relevant surfaces for that audience and access available; do not imply comprehensive coverage of all assistants.
- Client supplies factual product/service information and one responsible approver. Changes use existing page templates; substantial development and complex subject-matter research are separately scoped.

These are proposed boundaries for one viable example. The written quote can replace them. A larger company is not automatically excluded if the engagement itself is narrow and manageable.

### What happens each recurring month

| Work | Proposed bounded output | Why the buyer receives it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer and competitor research | Review one priority buyer-question/topic group using external search results, the relevant competitor pages and client questions. Confirm the next page improvement; review up to one relevant page from each of the three named competitors. | Keeps the work directed at demand and decision gaps, including queries the customer's own GSC cannot reveal. |
| Visibility and existing measurement review | Analyse the agreed question set's recorded observations and history; review existing organic/AI referral and conversion information where available for the scoped objective. With manual collection only, record the 20 observations. | Establishes a dated observation record and highlights missing information or changes worth investigating. |
| Implementation | Improve **one existing priority page**, or complete **one small technical task agreed for that cycle**. The page option can cover up to two substantive question/decision sections, normally within **600 added or materially revised words in total**, plus relevant title/heading/description corrections and up to three internal-link changes where these fit the agreed effort. | Produces a useful change, rather than only another audit. The limit is a scope boundary, not a word-count target or instruction to pad copy. |
| Verification within that improvement | Verify the edited content or corrected technical condition on the affected page/template. A sitewide change must fit an explicitly agreed small task, not be implied by “technical SEO included.” | Makes the work usable and checks that the implementation matches the intended change. This is included in the scoped improvement, not an additional unrestricted technical allowance. |
| Client report | One concise report containing changed URLs and evidence, observed metrics and limitations, blockers, and next month's recommendation. | Lets the customer see what was delivered and whether the next cycle is worth doing. |
| Review and revisions | One **20-minute review**, or the same questions answered asynchronously, plus **one consolidated revision round** on the scoped deliverable. | Provides a decision point without committing the founder to unlimited calls and edit cycles. |

**Implementation boundary:** the chosen content improvement or technical correction forms one agreed deliverable, estimated at about **1.5–2 hours including factual checks, implementation, QA and the consolidated revision**. This is not a promise of a full page rewrite plus arbitrary developer work. Choose the page improvement or named small technical task with the client before the cycle begins; agree any later replacement and its output before using the allocation. A substantial original-content or integration task needs its own scope. Do not silently replace the promised improvement with monitoring.

The public offer should emphasize the useful result—one agreed page improvement or small technical correction, verification, research and report. The more detailed limits belong in the representative scope and written quote. The business can price fixed deliverables while using time internally to avoid accepting an uneconomic commitment.

### How to keep monitoring honest

Ten questions should be genuine category, use-case, comparison and buying questions; they should not all be branded prompts constructed to make the customer appear. Choose them from the customer's offer, external demand research and sales questions, not only queries already appearing in GSC. Do not attach invented volume to natural-language questions.

Record question text, platform/surface, date, country or configured market, account state, whether web search was enabled, the result, citations/links, and relevant limitations. Keep the question set stable; if up to two questions are replaced to reflect a new priority, mark the change rather than presenting the new set as an uninterrupted trend.

With one manual run per question/surface, this is a **small directional snapshot**. It is not statistically reliable market share, a census of searches, or proof that the intervention caused any difference. A licensed tracker can collect repeated observations without multiplying human review time; verify its supported engines, workspaces, client permissions and actual billing before selecting it. A single-client subscription should not be assumed to permit unlimited agency accounts. More frequent collection improves the observation record but does not turn a constructed question set into population demand or causal evidence. Monitoring should remain a minority of the delivery budget so the agency has time to improve something.

Distinguish recommendations/mentions, linked citations, measured referrals, enquiries and attributed orders. Keep unavailable results labelled unavailable. When no eligible analytics or AI report exists, report the gap; do not substitute a proprietary readiness score as a business outcome.

## Month one: baseline plus a first useful change

**Month one should be different from a recurring month, not a full onboarding project added for free to the complete recurring allowance.** Explain this before the client signs.

For an already accessible, manageable website, the proposed first month includes:

1. Confirm one commercial objective, the priority URL set, market/language, factual sources and approver.
2. Review the five priority URLs and the relevant competitor pages; establish the ten buyer questions and a dated observation baseline.
3. Check available indexing, referral and conversion information; record what is and is not measurable. This is a measurement review, not rebuilding analytics.
4. Produce a short prioritised work list and implement the first **smaller** existing-page improvement that fits the remaining allocation.
5. Deliver the baseline/change record and next-month plan.

An indicative **5.5–6-hour** first month could allocate 0.5 hour to access/kickoff, 1.25 to buyer/competitor research and the priority-page review, 0.75 to measurement/technical checks, 0.75 to prompt observations, 1.0 to a first small page change, 0.75 to QA/report/review, and 0.5 to administration/contingency. These are planning assumptions, not measured production times. If access or source material is missing, this schedule does not become a promise to complete the impossible within six hours.

### When onboarding needs its own quote

Separate onboarding is appropriate when the engagement needs analytics or conversion-tracking implementation, a broad crawl and remediation plan, a platform migration, extensive schema work, a product feed, multiple markets, an access/security review, material factual research, or a substantial initial content backlog.

The client must see the onboarding scope, price and ongoing amount **before** agreeing. Do not advertise a $500 first invoice and reveal a compulsory setup fee afterwards. Equally, do not promise unlimited free setup to protect the headline price. The quote can show “initial work: separately scoped; ongoing work: $500/month from the agreed start,” or explicitly include a small baseline in month one.

If the site needs only a one-time repair and there is no credible continuing work list, sell that project and stop. Do not manufacture monthly changes after the useful work is complete. If a new build already paid for these foundations, use its existing checks and documentation rather than selling the same implementation again.

## Delivery-hour alternatives

| Option | Delivery model | Suitable use | Principal risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4–6 total hours/month** | The narrow example above: research, bounded monitoring, one existing-page improvement or agreed small technical task, included QA/revision, concise report and 20-minute review. | Existing editable sites, supplied facts, a narrow audience and one clear priority at a time. | Anything vaguely “unlimited” consumes the time reserved for actual improvement. |
| **7–10 total hours/month** | A broader research/review allowance, up to 15 questions across two surfaces (30 observations), two existing-page batches, one separately named small technical task, report and 30-minute review. | More valuable or complex accounts where the quote supports the work, or a deliberately limited pilot with recorded costs. | At $500, this can leave little contribution after labour and tools; owner capacity is consumed quickly. |
| **7–10 hours, content-focused alternative** | The same bounded research/monitoring/reporting, but replace the two-page batches and extra technical task with one new content item on an existing template, scoped from a client-supplied factual brief. | A specific missing buying question that cannot be answered well on an existing page. | Original research, stakeholder interviews, images, extensive revisions or highly regulated claims can push this well beyond the allowance. |

Do not promise two page improvements **and** a new long article **and** a separate developer backlog for $500 as a default. If the owner can economically allocate 7–10 hours, first increase implementation quality and research depth; multiplying prompt counts does not automatically create more customer value.

For the 4–6-hour option, an indicative recurring budget is:

| Activity | Working assumption |
| --- | ---: |
| Focused buyer/competitor research and prioritisation | 0.5–0.75 h |
| Existing measurement review and 20 observations | 0.75–1.0 h |
| One page improvement or technical task, including QA/revision | 1.5–2.0 h |
| Report | 0.5 h |
| Review | 0.33 h (20 minutes, rounded) |
| Administration | 0.25 h |
| Contingency | 0.5 h |
| **Total** | **Approximately 4.33–5.33 h** |

The estimate assumes a reused report format, manageable page complexity, working access, a factual brief, and an efficient way to capture the limited observation set. A human performing twenty slow manual searches and a novel technical investigation may need more time. Record actual effort during initial engagements and revise the quote or scope, not the quality standard.

## Illustrative unit economics

All figures in this section are hypothetical planning inputs. They are **not** PandaCodeGen's actual wage, margin, merchant rate, software bill or profit. Count founder time as a cost or opportunity cost; it is not free because no external invoice arrives.

Let:

- `P` = monthly fee, here $500.
- `H` = all recurring client delivery hours, including meetings, admin, QA and reporting.
- `C` = loaded direct hourly delivery cost or the owner's chosen cost of that time.
- `T` = direct tools/data cost allocated to the client.
- `f` = variable payment/collection cost as a share of revenue.

`Direct contribution = P − H × C − T − f × P`

`Contribution margin = Direct contribution / P`

Using **T = $50/client/month** and **f = 3%** purely for illustration:

| Hours H | Hourly cost C | Direct cost incl. tools/fees | Contribution at $500 | Contribution margin |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5 | $25 | $190 | $310 | 62% |
| 5 | $40 | $265 | $235 | 47% |
| 5 | $60 | $365 | $135 | 27% |
| 8 | $25 | $265 | $235 | 47% |
| 8 | $40 | $385 | $115 | 23% |
| 8 | $60 | $545 | −$45 | −9% |
| 10 | $25 | $315 | $185 | 37% |
| 10 | $40 | $465 | $35 | 7% |
| 10 | $60 | $665 | −$165 | −33% |

This is contribution before fixed business overhead, sales effort not already allocated, tax and other excluded costs—not net profit. If the loaded rate already includes a cost, do not add the same cost again. If founder capacity could be used for higher-value work, that opportunity matters even when cash contribution is positive.

For an illustrative target contribution margin `m`:

`Required fee P = (H × C + T) / (1 − f − m)`

At a **hypothetical 40%** target contribution and the same assumed tools/fees, 8 hours at $40/hour requires about **$649/month**; 10 hours requires about **$789**. At $60/hour, 8 hours requires about **$930**, and 10 hours about **$1,140**. These are scenario outputs, not recommended public pricing tiers. A real quote should also reflect onboarding, uncertainty, account management and the business's own margin goal.

At $500, with the same assumptions and 40% target, the available hours are about **5.88 at $40/hour**, or about **3.92 at $60/hour**. That illustrates why a $500 floor can be viable for a controlled small engagement but does not justify promising the same workload to every customer.

One further operational check: if the owner has only 40 hours per month available for all retainers after build commitments, holding 20% for uneven demand leaves 32 hours. At an average five hours/client that supports **six** accounts; at eight hours it supports **four**. This is an illustrative capacity calculation, not the owner's actual availability. Do not sell ten accounts assuming every month is the easiest month.

## When a midsize business needs a larger quote

Quote the work after identifying the demand and delivery context. Relevant triggers include:

- Several product/service lines or different buying audiences.
- US and UK coverage, additional languages, or geographically distinct offerings.
- More priority pages/templates, large catalogues, changing inventory or feeds.
- A larger or more frequently sampled question set, additional engines, repeated samples, or an enterprise reporting tool.
- New content, expert interviews, original research, reviews of sensitive claims or multiple approval layers.
- CMS development, inaccessible rendering, migrations, third-party integrations, staging/release coordination or a separate agency's implementation timetable.
- CRM/revenue attribution, multiple reporting systems, dashboards, internal stakeholder meetings or procurement requirements.
- Off-site authority work, partnerships or digital PR, if the business chooses to sell these separately and has the capability to deliver them.

Headcount and website page count alone should not decide the quote. A midsize business buying a narrowly scoped pilot can still fit the entry workload. A very small business needing everything rebuilt cannot.

## Proposed commercial boundaries and handoff

These are operating recommendations for the owner to accept or change, not legal terms being enacted by this report.

- **Rolling monthly, optional:** recommend no artificial long minimum term. The client can stop renewal before the next billing period under a clearly stated written deadline. A recommended three-month evaluation horizon is a planning suggestion, not a mandatory contract or promise of results by month three.
- **Scope before invoice:** name the initial work, recurring scope, fee, additional tools, access dependencies, approval responsibility and what counts as a larger separately quoted request.
- **One approval owner and one revision round:** consolidate stakeholder feedback. Rework caused by an agency error should not use up the client's paid change allowance.
- **Blocked work:** distinguish a delivered draft awaiting client approval from an undelivered task. If approval is delayed, work on another pre-agreed item where possible, report the blocker and offer to pause the next cycle when no useful work can proceed. Do not quietly collect successive fees for an inactive backlog or promise unlimited rollover capacity.
- **No automatic surprise expansions:** an urgent migration, analytics repair or full new article is not a “small monthly change.” Agree the output and price, or transparently replace an existing scoped item before work begins.
- **Client ownership and continuity:** deliver revised content, change history, question list, permitted observation exports, reports and configuration documentation. Use client-owned site and analytics accounts where appropriate; disclose any agency-owned subscription that is not transferable. Remove access on handoff when asked. Stopping a retainer should not remove already paid-for site changes.
- **Measurement, not guaranteed selection:** commit to the stated work and verification, not a citation count, ranking, lead volume or revenue outcome controlled by third parties and customer behaviour.
- **Review whether the retainer remains useful:** recommend continuation, a different scope, a one-time project or stopping based on the actual backlog and value—not because a monthly plan exists.

## What should be confirmed before the public page is rewritten

The owner needs to choose available monthly capacity, preferred contribution target, actual direct costs, whether original writing is included, who publishes changes on third-party platforms, supported reporting tools/surfaces and acceptable notice/approval boundaries. The research can continue while those answers are pending; these variables should not be silently invented in public copy.

The strongest defensible public starting offer, if the owner accepts its capacity requirements, is: **focused buyer research, a small named visibility/measurement set, one agreed existing-page improvement or small technical task with verification, and a monthly report/review—from $500, with wider requirements quoted separately.** That is concrete enough to evaluate and narrow enough to cost before committing.
