# AI SEO service page audit

**Recommendation: keep the commercial page, but revise the offer and buying journey before publishing it.** The page explains technical readability and demonstrates care with evidence. It does not yet give a customer enough information to choose a monthly AI SEO service. Its strongest position is implementation with founder involvement; its weakest point is the unexplained relationship between ordinary build quality and recurring marketing spend.

This review covers the [supplied draft](../ai-seo-page-for-review-2026-09-14.md), the current `/services/ai-seo` implementation, an existing local production render, stored measurement exports, external search intent and seven agency service-page benchmarks. Findings reflect 14 September 2026. The page remains a draft; the observations do not establish public rankings, future conversion performance or live production configuration.

## Overall assessment

| Perspective | Assessment | Main reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer with a website problem | Partly persuasive | Friendly contact and sensible technical examples, but unclear whether help requires a rebuild |
| Owner deciding how to spend money | Insufficiently defined | $500 is clearly a minimum, but there is no recognisable monthly scope to evaluate |
| Mid-sized business marketing lead | Not ready for procurement | Missing operating model, division of responsibility, sample output and engagement terms |
| Keyword/search-intent fit | Promising category, incomplete answer | Agency/service searches imply buying a programme; the opening primarily describes a build feature |
| Evidence and credibility | Strong disclosure; some corrections needed | Own-site observations are labelled, but relevance, aggregation and causality need attention |
| Technical SEO | Useful foundations present | Canonical, indexable local response and readable content exist; stale offer descriptions remain |
| Visual flow and CTAs | Coherent styling; inefficient sequence | Long hero pushes the mobile CTA down, and unrelated migration CTAs compete with the page's purpose |

These are editorial and commercial judgments, not measured customer-test scores.

## The buyer's unanswered question

**“What will you do for our business, what will we receive, and how will we judge whether to continue?”**

The three readability cards answer part of the implementation question. They do not explain what recurs every month. A business with an already server-rendered website could reasonably conclude that the main service has already been done by its developer. The page must explain the remaining paid responsibility, if one is genuinely offered.

The existing offer contains two established commitments: technical foundations included in a website build, and optional ongoing monitoring and agreed changes starting at $500/month. A third possible route—standalone help on an existing site—is suggested but not clearly defined. State which engagements are available independently. Do not make an existing-site buyer work through the FAQ to find out whether they are welcome.

The page should name concrete outputs only after the business confirms them. A representative entry scope is enough; artificial pricing tiers are unnecessary. Specify coverage, cadence, included implementation, reporting and boundaries. If a five-page example is used, give the actual work sold for it rather than making page count appear to be the sole pricing rule.

## Three buyer perspectives

### A customer

The low-pressure enquiry is a strength. “Ask us about your site” fits the visitor who has a concern but has not decided to buy. The founder reassurance and willingness to recommend a smaller job reduce uncertainty. The current modal already matches this approach and opens successfully in the local render.

The opening should first explain the service in business language and identify whether an existing site can be supported. The HTML/JavaScript explanation belongs immediately after that, as the reason behind the work. The current order requires the customer to understand crawler mechanics before understanding the offer.

Avoid equating a readable website with an AI-search acquisition programme. Accessibility may be the issue; it may also already be satisfactory. The first assessment should identify what matters for that business rather than presuppose a rendering problem.

### An owner or budget holder

The $500 floor is explicitly honest, but the scope remains ambiguous. Across twelve paid months it represents $6,000; that is arithmetic, not a minimum contract recommendation. A budget holder needs to understand what is being purchased repeatedly and the criteria for continuing, changing scope or stopping.

The page currently spends more effort defending the floor than demonstrating its value. Move the price explanation out of the long hero box. Put one real minimum-scope example, quote drivers and relevant terms together. State any setup or third-party costs if they exist; do not imply they are absent merely because the draft omits them.

The business also needs a delivery model that protects its capacity. Unbounded “monitoring and changes” can create conflicting expectations at a low starting price. Clarify the work allowance or bounded outputs, approval dependencies and treatment of larger requests. The audit does not determine whether $500 is profitable: staffing time, tool costs and actual scope were not provided.

### A mid-sized business

A marketing manager needs to fit the service around an existing CMS, internal developer, SEO agency and approval process. They may care less about having the cheapest floor than about whether work will be implemented and reported without disrupting established responsibilities.

Add a short account of how the actual team works: who owns the engagement, what access is needed, who changes the site, who supplies subject expertise, who approves releases and how findings become completed work. A redacted real scope or report would help an internal sponsor explain the purchase. State supported arrangements truthfully; do not invent headcount, SLAs, certifications, coverage or a dedicated account-management function.

Explain the commercial exit as well as the entry: actual minimum term, notice, retained access and handover of reports/configuration. “Optional retainer” establishes that a build does not require subscription; it says nothing about cancellation after subscribing.

## Competitor comparison

The seven-page benchmark includes WebFX, iPullRank, Impression, Omnius, Primo Interactive, Visionary and Ranking Tank. It spans broad agency programmes, enterprise strategy, specialist marketing and technical implementation. Their published claims describe their marketing and offers; they are not independent proof of results or superior conversion rates.

| Comparator | The useful pattern to borrow | What it resolves for this page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WebFX | Scope attached to price | What the recurring fee buys |
| iPullRank | Previews of named deliverables | What a manager can take into an internal decision |
| Impression | Case context and defined measurement | How work relates to a particular buying journey |
| Omnius | Activities separated from outputs | What the agency does versus what the client receives |
| Primo Interactive | Free technical check distinguished from paid work | Why a customer would need help after a scan |
| Visionary | Quote variables and engagement terms | How price and commitment are determined |
| Ranking Tank | Implementation and developer handoff made explicit | Who turns recommendations into changes |

See [the full comparison and primary links](competitor-comparison.md). The lesson is to show the work, not to imitate the largest firm's word count, guarantees or sales pressure.

**The draft's $1,500–$8,000+ market comparison should leave the hero.** Those programmes may include substantial content, strategy, authority work and reporting that the $500 offer does not promise. Calling the numbers agencies' own rates identifies the source category; it establishes neither retrievability nor comparable scope. A dated comparison can be useful later if it names providers, preserves currency and separates the work being compared. It cannot currently support an implied equivalent-service saving.

## Search intent and keyword strategy

One commercial URL remains appropriate. Use `/services/ai-seo` for the actual service, with AI SEO, AI search optimisation, AEO and GEO explained as related category language. Make the intended meaning explicit: “AI SEO” can also mean using AI tools for conventional SEO work. Searchers using the same short phrase are not necessarily buying the same service.

The reported US volumes and low difficulty scores remain **provisional historical evidence**, not fresh measurements or a revenue forecast. The tracked validation retains the four rows, but the referenced reconstructed raw captures are absent from this checkout. There is no fresh UK volume capture. Restore the preserved evidence before describing it as retrievable; do not recreate a response and label it original. DataForSEO's difficulty model estimates competition from backlink-derived scores, not a guarantee of this site's authority or ranking.[^1]

| Search family | Recommended treatment |
| --- | --- |
| AI SEO services / AI SEO agency | Primary commercial category, with the actual technical/ongoing offer clearly defined |
| AEO services / AI search optimisation services / GEO services | Same page; explain the work instead of creating acronym variants |
| AEO services pricing / AI SEO services cost | A substantive price-and-scope section on this page |
| AI search visibility audit | Distinguish technical scanning from a human visibility diagnostic; only claim the latter if actually offered |
| WordPress AI search optimisation services | Existing-site eligibility and implementation answer |
| AI SEO services for ecommerce | Relevant example and real catalogue/product-data scope, if supported |
| How to measure AI visibility / what is llms.txt | Existing educational articles, linked to the commercial page when help is relevant |

The full [keyword map](keyword-intent-map.md) distinguishes phrases with preserved estimates, phrases actually sampled in external search and unmeasured wording hypotheses. It includes long-tail candidates and the gaps in US/UK evidence. Search-result appearances are not quantitative demand measurements.

The `seo-aeo` cluster already points to the service page; the old editorial-policy hub gap is fixed. However, “no cannibalisation” is too absolute. The existing AEO playbook overlaps the implementation argument and contains service language in metadata. Preserve an instructional role for the article and a buying role for the service page, then inspect query-to-page performance after publication. Do not remove useful educational content or redirect the playbook merely because the new commercial page exists.

Google's current guidance also warns against creating pages for every possible query variation. Cover real customer needs naturally rather than forcing awkward long-tail phrases into every heading.[^2]

## Evidence and technical corrections

The stored exports support **9,843 AI-feature impressions** and **157,920 Web-search impressions** over matching dates. The ratio rounds to **6.2%**. Keep those observations and the relevant limits. They establish visibility in Google's AI features, not that the proposed service caused it or that equivalent buyers will generate sales.

The “57%” sentence should change. The hosting article has 5,583 page impressions, while the AI page table totals 9,899 and the property chart totals 9,843. A page numerator divided by a property denominator is not a clean share. State the page count directly, or use the clearly labelled page-table share of about 56%. The archive has 92 daily rows; the evidence registry's 91-day description is also off by one. Google documents the aggregation distinction.[^3]

Retain the three central caveats: impressions are not visits/sales, the audience is largely unrelated to buying this service, and the report covers Google only. Put a relevant work sample ahead of the large visibility number. A clear example of a problem, delivered change and verifiable result is more useful than displaying a broader historical count without an intervention.

Several phrases need narrower wording: availability in HTML does not assure it is read; useful images should not be described as unreadable by everything; help with citation eligibility differs from guaranteeing a citation; and a Lighthouse check is not a ranking factor. The detailed [claims review](claims-and-measurement.md) verifies these distinctions against current primary documentation, including the real Generative AI report, optional llms.txt audit, separate search/training crawlers and retired Google FAQ rich results.

The implemented metadata, related-service card and llms.txt summary still imply that there is no monthly service. Fix all three together. The rendered title omits “services”; adding it would make the commercial purpose clearer, while the description should accurately mention both included build foundations and optional ongoing work. Meta keywords cannot substitute for that clarity.

## Information flow, design and CTAs

The visual style is coherent: readable typography, restrained colours and a clear form. The problem is hierarchy. At a 390 × 844 mobile viewport, the main enquiry button begins around **985 px** down the document, after crawler explanation and the extended price defence. On desktop at 1440 × 1000 it begins around **804 px**. The first-visit consent panel further reduces the visible area. Moving the useful next action earlier should be a layout decision; it is not a claim of a measured conversion lift.

The whole rendered page is about **12,459 px on mobile** and **7,349 px on desktop** in this local build. Part of that length is the full FAQ, related-services grid and global footer. The footer introduces another large migration pitch after the AI SEO close, which is absent from the supplied “full rendered text.” Keep the service-specific close and suppress or contextualise the competing global pitch on this route.

Recommended sequence:

1. **Service, customer problem and fit.** Short category statement, existing-site eligibility where true, concise benefit and enquiry CTA.
2. **What you receive.** Separate included build work from the optional monthly engagement. Show the real entry scope.
3. **Relevant work example.** Actual implementation, sample output and relationship disclosure.
4. **How delivery works.** Initial assessment, written scope, implementation, verification and the optional ongoing cycle; actual responsibility and timing.
5. **Monthly scope and pricing.** Minimum scope, quote drivers, exclusions and terms, without an unsupported savings comparison.
6. **Measurement.** Work completed, observed visibility and attributable enquiries/orders kept distinct.
7. **Buying FAQs.** Existing CMS/team, content responsibility, commitment, access and free-versus-paid differences.
8. **One coherent close.** Enquiry first, call second, and a technical-check link if the copy invites it.

“Ask us about your site” should stay. The form already asks “What would you like help with?” and submits with “Send your question”; the old modal mismatch is fixed. Consider an optional website URL field and a “Question or details” label. Keep the basic enquiry short.

The local “Book a call” button has no configured calendar destination and falls back through the shared integration to the enquiry form. This is a local configuration/fallback mismatch, not proof that production booking is broken. Align calendar configuration and check the actual published destination before claiming the booking journey is verified. See [visual and technical observations](visual-and-technical.md).

## A copy direction that preserves the actual offer

These examples are editorial proposals, not additions to the service scope.

**Title:** AI SEO Services & Answer Engine Optimisation | PandaCodeGen

**H1:** AI SEO services, from technical foundations to ongoing care

**Opening:** We make important website content accessible to search systems and AI assistants. The technical foundations are included when we build your site. Optional ongoing monitoring and agreed changes start at $500 a month, with a written scope before work begins.

**CTA:** Ask us about your site

**Reassurance:** A founder replies, usually within one business day. Every message gets an answer, whether or not it turns into a quote.

**Guarantee boundary:** We can check and improve the parts of your site we control. We cannot guarantee that an AI service will recommend your business or cite a particular page. Your scope will explain the work we will deliver and how it will be checked.

The next line must explain existing-site eligibility, once confirmed. The pricing section still needs real deliverables; the sample opening cannot solve that business decision by itself.

## Answers to the draft's four review questions

1. **Is $500 clearly a floor?** Yes. Scope ambiguity can still create surprise. Define a real entry engagement rather than repeating that larger sites cost more.
2. **Is attribution to agencies' published prices enough?** No. It needs named retrievable sources and comparable scope; the current juxtaposition implies more than it demonstrates.
3. **Do the evidence limits build trust or undercut the figure?** They build trust. The figure is being used too heavily as service proof. Improve relevance and correct aggregation while keeping the limits.
4. **Is there overclaiming or excessive hedging?** Both: absolute readability/image claims overreach, while a blanket refusal of targets/citation help makes the offer sound less useful than bounded implementation actually is.
