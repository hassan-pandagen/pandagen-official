# AI SEO page: claims and measurement review

The page's evidence discipline is worth keeping. The largest remaining risk is asking a modest technical-accessibility offer and a historical visibility count to imply a broader acquisition service. The findings below distinguish a verified observation, a technical prerequisite, a business objective and a guaranteed result.

## Claim decisions

| Draft claim or implication | Decision | Recommended treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content is present in the HTML sent by the server | Supported for this local page: the existing production HTML contains the main content and FAQ answers | Show a real completed implementation or verification record. Do not generalise one template check to every page on a customer's site. |
| Therefore the page is read either way | Too absolute | Say important content is available in the initial HTML, including to a fetcher that does not render JavaScript. Access, discovery, indexing and source selection remain separate. |
| Important facts should be text rather than only images | Sound practical recommendation | Remove the assertion that nothing can read images. Keep useful images and provide essential facts in accessible text. |
| Every heading should be the question it resolves | Useful in some sections, not an AI requirement | Use descriptive headings that match the reader's task. A commercial heading such as “Monthly scope and pricing” is clearer than forcing every section into a question. |
| 9,843 Google AI-feature impressions, 6.2% of Web impressions | Supported by stored archive arithmetic and current report definitions | Keep the property, dates, report name and Google-only scope. Describe this as observed visibility, not proof that a build or retainer caused it. |
| One article accounts for 57% of the total | Aggregation mismatch | Prefer the actual 5,583 page-impression count and state that it dominates the page table. See calculation below. |
| Panda Patches attribution runs end to end | The repository describes an attribution implementation; order-level validation was not available in this audit | Link to a matching method/example. Keep the co-founder ownership disclosure. Do not add the registry's revenue figure without reviewing its source and attribution rules. |
| Competitors charge $1,500–$8,000+ while this starts at $500 | Not a defensible like-for-like comparison in its current placement | Remove from the hero. Named sources alone cannot establish equivalent work. Publish a real entry scope first. |
| No citation, ranking or traffic target of any kind | Commercially overrestrictive | Exclude guarantees. Define review objectives and measurement without promising that an engine will select the business. |
| The answer to whether the agency can get a business cited is simply no | Conflates help with a guarantee | Explain the work the agency controls, the intended purpose, and the limit on promising selection. Remove the accusation aimed at anyone answering differently. |
| llms.txt is not a Google ranking factor | Supported by Google's current, explicit guidance | Link to that guidance; keep this detail below the main buying argument. |
| Cursor and GitHub Copilot read llms.txt | Insufficient evidence for the broad, unqualified product claim | Name a documented workflow/version or use narrower language about compatible tools using a supplied documentation index. Do not imply automatic use during public web discovery. |
| PageSpeed has three checks and scores a compliant llms.txt | Needs version and scope qualification | The category is experimental; a missing file returning 404 is documented as N/A. A count in a dated measurement is not a timeless product specification. |
| GPTBot/ClaudeBot/PerplexityBot log hits show whether it is being used | A log hit can show a request, not use in an answer | Include search-specific agents, distinguish training from search, validate identity where possible, and do not equate fetching with ranking, citation or conversion. |
| Adding llms.txt costs nothing | Ambiguous commercial wording | If true, say it is included without a separate charge. Creating and maintaining a file still involves work. |

The quoted fragments above are from the supplied local draft, not competitor text. The decisions are reviewer judgments except where a source or stored calculation is explicitly identified.

## Current technical guidance

Google's newer AI optimisation guide says its Search systems ignore llms.txt for visibility and rankings. It also says that special AI prose, forced content chunking and special structured data are unnecessary. Technical accessibility remains useful, but original, helpful information and ordinary Search eligibility matter too. The page should present server-delivered content as part of competent implementation, with a clear explanation of the additional recurring service.[^1]

Google can process JavaScript. Consequently, this page should not suggest that adopting Next.js, rebuilding WordPress or passing one accessibility test is a general prerequisite for appearing in AI answers. The older AI-features guide documents crawling, indexing and snippet eligibility, with no assurance that a qualifying page will be served.[^2] The newer Search Console inclusion control is also relevant to an actual technical assessment; it is a site setting, not a purchasable ranking signal.[^3]

The Lighthouse llms.txt audit treats a missing file with HTTP 404 as not applicable. Its experimental Agentic Browsing category offers diagnostic checks rather than a Search-ranking score.[^4][^5] The draft may accurately describe a particular dated run, but “one of three checks” requires a pinned version and actual report. A pass cannot substantiate increased recommendations or sales.

OpenAI separates OAI-SearchBot for search from GPTBot for possible model-training use and ChatGPT-User for user-triggered retrieval.[^6] Anthropic similarly distinguishes Claude-SearchBot, ClaudeBot and Claude-User.[^7] Perplexity documents its own search crawler and user agent.[^8] A practical audit should test the controls relevant to the customer's goal, including robots rules and CDN access; changing a user-agent header in a local request does not establish that a verified service bot successfully visited.

Cursor's Origin API documentation exposes an llms.txt reference for agents. That supports the narrower claim that a documentation index can be useful in a tool workflow; it does not establish that all Cursor or Copilot activity automatically reads a commercial site's file.[^9] The llms.txt specification itself describes a convention, not a universal adoption guarantee.[^10]

FAQ answers are still useful for customers. However, Google ended FAQ rich results on May 7, 2026 and subsequently removed the feature documentation. Adding FAQPage markup should not be presented as a current rich-result opportunity or expected CTR increase.[^11] The local page's answers are visible and match its FAQ data source; their customer value does not depend on that retired display feature.

## The Search Console evidence

The [stored AI export](../../../GSC/pandacodegen.com-Performance-on-Search-Generative-AI-Features-2026-09-13.zip) and [stored Web export](../../../GSC/pandacodegen.com-Performance-on-Search-2026-09-13.zip) cover **12 June–11 September 2026: 92 daily observations inclusive**. The detailed archive checks appear in [buyer-and-implementation.md](buyer-and-implementation.md).

| Calculation | Result | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| AI Chart.csv impressions | 9,843 | Property-level chart observations |
| Web Chart.csv impressions, same dates | 157,920 | Comparable Web chart total |
| 9,843 / 157,920 × 100 | 6.2329% | Correctly rounded to 6.2%; do not add the two totals |
| AI Pages.csv impressions | 9,899 | Exported page-level table observations |
| Hosting article's page impressions | 5,583 | A page-level observation |
| 5,583 / 9,843 × 100 | 56.7205% | Arithmetic behind 57%, but mixed aggregation |
| 5,583 / 9,899 × 100 | 56.3996% | About 56% of the exported page-table impressions |

Google documents the difference between property-level chart totals and page-level table aggregation, and identifies the Generative AI Search report as an impressions report for AI Overviews and AI Mode.[^12] Its existence and impressions-only scope are current facts; it would be wrong to reject this evidence by relying on older descriptions of Search Console.

The registry's “91-day window” should be corrected if retained. The public draft uses dates rather than this incorrect day count. The 6.2% comparison is valid as a chart-to-chart ratio, subject to matching filters. It is not a share of people, clicks, qualified prospects or orders.

A safer proof block, using the available observations:

> **What we have measured on our own site**
>
> Our stored Search Console export records 9,843 impressions in Google's AI features between 12 June and 11 September 2026. That is 6.2% of the Web-search impressions recorded over the same dates. The report measures appearances, not visits or sales. A hosting-bandwidth article recorded 5,583 page impressions and dominates the page table, so this is not evidence of demand for our services. These observations cover Google's features only and do not establish that a particular site change caused them.

The local archives support their contents and calculations; this review did not authenticate them through a fresh account export. This is an evidence boundary, not a suggestion that the figures are fabricated.

## Panda Patches evidence and relevance

The linked [Panda Patches case](../../../src/app/work/panda-patches/page.tsx) currently demonstrates engineering and migration work. Give the service-page attribution statement an exact destination explaining the implemented measurement. Keep the relationship as “a business owned by our co-founder”; “our own company” blurs the legal distinction recorded in the case.

Before publishing order/revenue figures, establish the attribution window, first/last-touch rule, treatment of self-reported discovery, deduplication, missing referrers, refunds and whether totals are gross or net. A redacted completed example can demonstrate how a referral is associated with an order without exposing customer information. Do not infer incremental revenue caused by AI SEO from orders labelled with an AI source. Do not call mixed referrer/self-report attribution a proven lower bound without assessing possible over-attribution as well as missing attribution.

## A useful client reporting model

This is a proposed reporting structure, not a commitment that it is included at $500. Confirm the actual scope before publishing it.

| Layer | A useful client-facing record | Limitation to state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivered work | Prioritised issue, affected URL/template, change, approval, completion date and verification | Completing a technical task does not establish business lift |
| Technical access | Important content in the initial response; relevant crawl/index controls; successful verification | Availability does not establish retrieval or recommendation |
| Google AI visibility | Same-window GSC observations on relevant commercial/content pages | Google-specific; impressions are not referral visits |
| Sampled AI answers | Fixed questions, country/language, date, engine, cited URL and stored response | A changing sample, not the total audience or a platform-wide market share |
| Referred visits | Recorded source and landing page, with attribution limitations | Missing referrers and tracking coverage affect counts |
| Business outcomes | Qualified enquiries/orders linked under a documented rule | Attribution does not demonstrate incremental causation |
| Next decision | What to change, leave alone, investigate or stop based on the evidence | Avoid implying that another paid month necessarily produces growth |

This lets a customer evaluate a retainer while preserving honest limits. Removing guarantees does not require removing goals, decisions or accountability.
