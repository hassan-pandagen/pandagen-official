# AI SEO service page: competitor and commercial comparison

Reviewed 14 September 2026 against [the supplied rendered draft](../ai-seo-page-for-review-2026-09-14.md). This is a commercial-content benchmark, not a ranking, conversion-rate study, procurement recommendation, or verification of agency results. Seven agency service pages were researched across US, UK, European and remote providers. Established full-service agencies were included alongside narrower technical studios because PandaCodeGen needs to understand both buyer expectations and realistic differentiation.

The strongest gap is **offer definition**. The draft clearly explains why basic technical readability should be included in a website build. It does not yet explain an independently purchasable AI SEO engagement well enough for an owner or marketing manager to assess the optional $500 monthly minimum. A buyer can understand the ethics and still be unable to buy the service confidently.

## What was checked and how much confidence to give it

The observations below come from primary agency pages and linked pricing, case-study, technology or enquiry pages accessed on 14 September 2026. They describe what those agencies publish, not verified capabilities or independently established causation. No agency was contacted and no form was submitted. Rankings, paid-ad placement, review authenticity, company headcount and conversion performance were not measured. This comparison does not prove that a particular layout ranks or converts better.

Six service pages were directly accessible. The WebFX comparison uses indexed content from the exact primary service URL, including its pricing and deliverables; its linked case-study page was directly accessible. Indexed observations may differ in freshness from the live page. Competitor browser appearance, responsive behaviour and interactive form completion are outside this commercial-content comparison.

## Seven commercial benchmarks

### 1. WebFX: a managed program with a tangible commercial scope

**Primary page:** [AI search optimization services](https://www.webfx.com/seo/services/ai-search-optimization/). US full-service comparator.

The page progresses from AI discovery and business visibility to client examples, its tracking platform, process, benefits, pricing, detailed service components and FAQs. It publishes a **$3,000/month starting price**, with research, content and visibility software identified beside it. A further matrix specifies prompt and engine coverage. Proposal CTAs recur throughout. The offer encompasses audits, strategy, ongoing SEO/content/authority work and reporting; it is substantially broader than serving HTML plus optional maintenance.

Its [10X Materials case](https://www.webfx.com/portfolio/case-studies/10x-materials-omniseo/) connects an industrial buyer problem to content creation, outreach and reporting, identifies the client and shows outcome graphics. This is an agency-published account, not independent verification of an AI-specific effect.

**Useful lesson:** put the work and its boundaries beside the price. **Do not copy:** dominance language or aggregate client numbers as a substitute for evidence relevant to PandaCodeGen's own offer. The indexed matrix is not proof every item is included at the starting fee.

### 2. iPullRank: enterprise strategy a manager can explain internally

**Primary page:** [AI Search Strategy Program](https://ipullrank.com/ai-search-strategy-program). US enterprise/mid-market positioning.

The opening identifies an executive audience and a **$15,000/month starting price**. Flow: commercial purpose, deliverable previews, detailed explanations, proof, named specialists and a discovery-call close. It distinguishes the core strategy materials from audit/roadmap add-ons. Content and measurement plans connect execution to responsibilities, business objectives and leadership decisions. This gives an internal sponsor something more concrete to buy than generalized visibility.

The page provides self-published AI visibility examples. Some neighboring percentage headlines and before/after numbers do not reconcile as one simple calculation; their definitions need explanation before reuse. The discovery CTA resolves to the service page's own section in the fetched representation; a completed scheduling flow was not tested.

**Useful lesson:** show what a buyer receives and how their team uses it. **Do not copy:** enterprise complexity, scarcity framing, or this fee as evidence of what a small technical maintenance offer should cost.

### 3. Impression: connect technical work to content, brand and business measurement

**Primary page:** [Generative Engine Optimisation agency](https://www.impressiondigital.com/generative-engine-optimisation-agency/). UK agency with a US presence.

The sequence is category explanation, buyer relevance, client evidence, approach, measurement, contributing services, testimonials, FAQs and contact. It explains brand/content/technical work as an integrated program and distinguishes prompt tracking as a visibility proxy from referral and conversion reporting. No public fee was observed on the service page. Contact is framed as a discussion with an expert.

The linked [Tensar case](https://www.impressiondigital.com/our-work/tensar-geo/) names the business, explains its long buying cycle, describes interventions and states tools and a twelve-month window. Its GSC clicks are across keywords associated with AI Overviews; they are not demonstrated clicks directly from an Overview. The case is self-published and does not isolate a causal GEO lift.

**Useful lesson:** show why the work matters to a particular buying journey, with measurement definitions. **Do not copy:** broad statements about how all LLMs use particular technical signals without separate primary-system evidence.

### 4. Omnius: narrow customer fit and activities paired with outputs

**Primary page:** [AI SEO services](https://www.omnius.so/ai-seo-services). European specialist positioning with UK market presence.

The page identifies SaaS/fintech buyers, introduces AI discovery, then lists workstreams with separate activities and outputs. It follows with its technology proposition, sector focus, case links, named customer testimonials, FAQs and enquiry/call paths. The rendered contact fields include name, business email, website and an optional message. No public service price was observed.

The linked [technology page](https://www.omnius.so/technology) explains its measurement model and explicitly discusses nondeterminism, synthetic sampling and attribution limits. This gives procurement readers a methodology destination beyond the main pitch. Its proprietary-technology and uniqueness claims remain self-published; the service page also includes categorical crawler/schema assertions that should not be imported as technical facts.

**Useful lesson:** clarify who the offer serves, and separate tasks from the artifacts the customer gets. **Do not copy:** exclusivity language, universal technical claims or ordinary SEO outcomes as proof of an isolated AI-search contribution.

### 5. Primo Interactive: a close comparison to development plus ongoing service

**Primary page:** [AEO services](https://www.primointeractive.com/services/aeo). UK web/software and marketing provider.

The page explicitly separates an initial audit and ongoing retainer from its wider services. It moves through rationale, service components, a four-stage process, a named dealership engagement, tools, FAQs and contact. It includes bot-policy work, structure, content and brand monitoring. It explains that its free checker cannot replace competitive context, answer-accuracy review and prioritization. No public AEO fee was observed.

The dealership example discloses an existing website/software relationship, but provides no numerical baseline or reproducible citation sample. The [quote destination](https://www.primointeractive.com/contacts/request-quote) loaded; the fetched representation did not expose usable form-field detail, so friction was not scored.

**Useful lesson:** explain the specific gap between a free technical check and paid work. **Do not copy:** implying the presence of a named case alone establishes measured improvement, or assuming every knowledge-base intervention is appropriate for every client.

### 6. Visionary: budget qualification, scope drivers and engagement terms

**Primary page:** [GEO agency](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/seo/generative-engine-optimisation-agency). UK specialist comparator.

The page foregrounds senior contact and no-contract positioning, then presents proof, mechanisms, workstreams, measurement, pricing and FAQs. It publishes **£1,300–£2,500/month** and connects variation to prompt coverage, content volume, technical work and entity work. The opening enquiry asks for name/email, with company optional. The [linked package page](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/seo/seo-packages) itemizes quantities, reporting cadence and rolling monthly terms.

The main page mixes general SEO and paid-media proof around the GEO offer. These are not interchangeable with AI-specific outcomes. Its detailed price/quantity schedule is a vendor assertion; this review did not establish its economics, capacity or delivery record.

**Useful lesson:** a price floor is easier to interpret when variables and exit terms are explicit. **Do not copy:** untested ROI projections, generalized claims about other agencies or unrelated channel results as evidence that GEO caused revenue.

### 7. Ranking Tank: translate technical competence into a purchasable specification

**Primary page:** [Generative engine optimization services](https://rankingtank.com/services/generative-engine-optimization). Remote technical studio; local market share was not established.

The sequence is retrieval/extraction/citation framing, technical explanation, measurement limits, explicit inclusions, relationship to technical SEO, FAQs and an audit CTA. It names concrete outputs such as a bot-access policy, template-level formatting specifications, developer tickets and a prioritized rollout. Implementation can involve its developers or the customer's. No public fee or detailed client case was observed on this service page; there is a link to its wider work.

The audit CTA fetch failed in this review, so its destination experience is unverified. Its confident statements about shared engine behavior and what citation samples prove should be treated as marketing assertions requiring independent technical validation.

**Useful lesson:** developer ownership and deliverable names can make a technical offer commercially legible without enterprise packaging. **Do not copy:** skepticism expressed as certainty that all other measurement is invalid.

## What this means for the supplied draft

The following are reviewer judgments about PandaCodeGen's draft, informed by the comparison. They are not claims that a given competitor's approach produces higher conversions.

| Buyer question | What the draft currently answers | What is still missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can I hire you without replacing my website? | Existing server-rendered pages may only need edits; the service is repeatedly described as part of the build. | A clear standalone path for an existing site, what is assessed and whether implementation on that platform is offered. |
| What do I buy for $500? | Optional monitoring and changes, scoped before a quote. | The smallest real scope sold at that price: coverage, frequency, work allowance or bounded outputs, tools and exclusions. |
| Why should my business prioritize this? | AI assistants need readable content. | The actual business problem and a qualification process that determines whether AI search matters to this customer's buyers. |
| What happens first? | Send a URL and a founder looks at it. | What comes back, when, what is free, and what requires a paid engagement. |
| Who does the changes? | The agency builds readable sites and transfers code/accounts. | Whether ongoing work includes shipping edits, advising the internal team, or both; approval and release ownership. |
| What do I show my finance director? | An impression count, disclaimers and a starting fee. | A sample scope/report, priorities, reporting cadence and a way to judge continuation without promising revenue. |
| How do we stop? | The retainer is optional. | Minimum term, cancellation/notice, account/data access and what is handed over at exit. Optional purchase does not define cancellation. |
| Why you instead of our existing agency? | Founder access, technical implementation and avoiding unnecessary rebuilds. | How the engagement complements an existing SEO/content team and the particular implementation problem PandaCodeGen is best equipped to solve. |

### Owner/customer perspective

The default CTA and reassurance are good. A visitor can ask a question without presenting a developed project or scheduling a meeting. Keep that conversational entry point.

The page's explanation currently begins with crawler mechanics, then immediately spends substantial space defending a price comparison. An owner with a working WordPress site can reasonably conclude that this is a rebuild pitch they do not need. That interpretation is reinforced when the FAQ says an editing-only case may not need the agency at all.

The smaller-job honesty is worth preserving, but do not turn it into accidental rejection of the exact buyer who wants a modest improvement engagement. State whether PandaCodeGen will perform the editing/implementation job, help their existing developer, or only advise. If the answer is not yet settled, that is a business decision to settle before final copy.

### Business-owner perspective

The $500 minimum can work as a budget filter. Repeating that it is a floor does not explain value. A owner needs a minimum viable engagement they can recognize: its inputs, outputs and recurring work. This need not mean rigid Bronze/Silver/Gold tiers or invented page quotas. It can be one representative scope that is genuinely available, with clear conditions that increase the price.

The build and monthly service should be separate decision units. A buyer should be able to identify which technical standards are included once, what ongoing work is optional, and what work is separately commissioned. Price comparisons with comprehensive agencies obscure those boundaries instead of clarifying them.

### Mid-sized business/marketing-manager perspective

The draft is less ready for a mid-sized business than for a small founder-led site. A manager already has a CMS, internal stakeholders, an SEO provider, content approval, brand rules, release dates and reporting obligations. They need to know whether this agency can fit into that setup. They do not necessarily need a larger team or a higher price; they need a credible operating arrangement.

Add the applicable arrangements that actually exist: named day-to-day owner, supported CMS/platforms, required access, client review responsibilities, implementation route, report cadence, scope-change treatment and handover. A concise sample scope and report are more useful than a generic claim of enterprise readiness. Do not invent service levels, security certifications, procurement accommodations or capacity.

## The price comparison should change

**Attributing a range to unnamed agencies does not solve the comparison problem.** A retrievable source answers where a price came from. It does not demonstrate that the quoted service is comparable to PandaCodeGen's scope.

The draft places the third-party range beside the $500 minimum, creating a savings impression before comparable inclusions exist. The ongoing fee is explained twice, but no minimum monthly output is named. A buyer may understandably anchor at $500 and expect the service breadth associated with the AI SEO category.

Recommended treatment: remove the market range from the hero. Publish the actual entry scope and the variables used to quote. If a market comparison is genuinely useful, place a dated, linked comparison lower on the page and distinguish technical maintenance, audits, implementation and broader content/authority programs. Use original currencies. Do not calculate a market average, universal minimum or percentage saving from this small, deliberately mixed sample. The upper end in the draft is not a ceiling, but that caveat still does not make unlike programs comparable.

## Make the evidence demonstrate the work being sold

The limitations beside PandaCodeGen's own figure are valuable. They disclose the audience mismatch, absent click/sales inference and Google-only coverage. Removing those limitations to resemble competitors would weaken the page.

The current problem is the claim the statistic is being asked to support. A total count can demonstrate an observed appearance during a period, subject to validation of its source. It does not by itself show that these three technical practices caused the appearances, that paid retainers improve them, or that a mid-sized customer's buying-intent queries will perform similarly.

A stronger commercial evidence block would show a relevant problem, the actual change made, the observable before/after technical condition and the remaining uncertainty. For example, a real raw-HTML versus rendered-page comparison on a relevant template can substantiate an accessibility repair. A real reporting sample can show how monitoring becomes a decision. A documented attribution implementation can substantiate that tracking was built; it does not automatically substantiate revenue lift.

Keep related-party disclosure beside Panda Patches. If no independent AI-service client result exists, say so without apologizing for the entire offer. Sell the demonstrated implementation capability and the bounded work available now. Do not label work in progress as a proven acquisition program.

## Recommended information sequence

This is an architecture recommendation, not approved final copy or invented service scope.

1. **Identify the commercial service and customer problem.** Make the relationship between AI SEO/AEO and the particular technical service clear, while explaining the benefit in business language.
2. **State fit and available engagement routes.** Existing site, new build, or ongoing work; include only the routes genuinely supported.
3. **Explain what you receive.** A short inclusion table or concrete deliverable list, with build work separated from recurring work.
4. **Show a relevant worked example or sample artifact.** Keep source, dates, method and relationship disclosure close to any result.
5. **Explain delivery.** Initial assessment, agreed scope, implementation/review, verification and optional continuation. Actual timelines and responsibilities should come from the business.
6. **Explain pricing and scope drivers.** Define the minimum and what adds work; link formal engagement details where appropriate.
7. **Explain measurement and boundaries.** What will be inspected, what can be observed, and what cannot be guaranteed.
8. **Answer purchase objections.** Existing CMS, existing SEO agency, copy ownership, contract/exit, required access and the difference between the free check and paid work.
9. **Close with the question CTA.** A call can remain a secondary route for buyers ready to discuss scope.

Do not expand to match competitor word counts. The draft can remain relatively short if the repeated pricing defense is replaced by distinct answers. Useful headings help a buyer find a decision; they should not all become keyword variants or questions solely to resemble AI-search templates.

## CTA and qualification recommendations

**Keep:** the low-commitment question CTA, founder response reassurance and free-check option. These match the user's stated preference and the exploratory nature of many visitors.

**Clarify:** the free check is a technical diagnostic, not a measurement of how frequently a brand is recommended across AI systems. Explain what sending a URL to the founder adds: interpretation, fit assessment or a scoped recommendation, if that is what is genuinely delivered.

**Avoid promising a deliverable that the click does not produce.** The button, opened form, submit label, confirmation and subsequent response should describe the same action. A question CTA should open an enquiry form and a call CTA should lead to the call pathway. The companion [visual review](visual-and-technical.md) confirms the corrected enquiry modal and identifies a local calendar-configuration fallback that still needs production verification.

**Qualify through useful context:** URL, the question/problem, target market, important products/services and whether an internal team handles changes can help scope a response. Keep optional fields optional until needed. Do not copy larger agencies' budget/phone-heavy forms merely because those agencies use them.
