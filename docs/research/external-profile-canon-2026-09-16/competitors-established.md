# Established technical agencies: external directory profile research

Research date: **2026-09-16**. Scope: Bejamas, Pagepro, Naturaily, Blazity, and FocusReactive; each agency's Clutch and DesignRush profile was opened through the web reader. These are primary sources for what the directories publish, not independent verification of agency claims. Search snippets were used for discovery, not as the sole evidence. Cached crawl freshness varies; the oldest returned page was Blazity's DesignRush profile (six months). No profiles were edited.

This is a messaging and information-architecture comparison. Directory prominence, badges, star ratings, review text, and paid placements do **not** establish better lead conversion, search rankings, delivery quality, or causation. Proof below means **proof presented by the profile**. Competitor certifications, project counts, results, and partnerships remain attributed claims unless separately verified.

## Bejamas

**Clutch tagline:** “Migrate to a website your team can run.”

- **Opening and offer:** Addresses marketing/digital teams managing complex websites without an internal senior web function. The sequence is assessment, build, and ongoing operation, with migration continuity, editor independence, and written commercial decisions. Two entry routes are a fixed-price discovery/audit and a quarterly team retainer.
- **Services and qualification:** Web development, commerce, design, and related delivery categories; minimum project $10,000+, hourly band $50–$99.
- **Proof and CTA:** The description claims 200+ projects, 20+ continuing retainers, named clients, and ecosystem partnerships. Clutch supplies website, contact, custom-proposal, and question actions; the description adds concrete engagement choices. [Bejamas on Clutch](https://clutch.co/profile/bejamas)

**DesignRush tagline:** “We build fast sites & apps.”

- **Opening and offer:** Starts with speed, scalability, and security for static sites/apps, then lists tools and Jamstack/PWA specialties. This reads as a technical capability pitch rather than the buyer-role framing on Clutch.
- **Proof and CTA:** Backlinko and Armorblox portfolio entries plus a named client list; website and contact actions. Budget $10,000–$25,000; $75/hour. The page's team-size field also differs from Clutch. [Bejamas on DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/profile/bejamas)

**PandaCodeGen lesson (inference):** Explain who owns delivery, what the client can manage after launch, and how the work starts. Maintain a shared current description across directories; copying a stack list alone loses the more specific buyer story.

## Pagepro

**Clutch tagline:** “NEXT.JS, EXPO & SANITY DEVELOPMENT AGENCY”

- **Opening and offer:** Leads with narrow platform expertise, then promises a controlled development process. The service menu makes that specialization practical through application builds, audits, migration, discovery, hosting-cost work, and post-launch support.
- **Qualification:** Minimum project $25,000+, $50–$99/hour; a separate monthly software package starts at $11,200.
- **Proof and CTA:** Published recognition claims and Clutch verification/review modules accompany website, contact, custom-proposal, and question actions. [Pagepro on Clutch](https://clutch.co/profile/pagepro)

**DesignRush tagline:** “Next.js, Expo & Headless CMS Development Agency”

- **Opening and offer:** Targets organizations that have outgrown their website setup. Explains content architecture, migration safeguards, technical discovery, performance, and takeover/rescue work. Explicit buyer-fit bullets cover growing companies, complex marketing content, multiple regions/brands, and legacy CMS migration.
- **Proof and CTA:** Portfolio cards include project type, budget, duration, and year. The page presents recognition claims, a verification label, and separately labeled DesignRush/Google reviews. Website/contact actions are the next steps. Budget $10,000–$25,000; $50/hour, differing from the Clutch minimum. [Pagepro on DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/profile/pagepro)

**PandaCodeGen lesson (inference):** Pair a short specialization line with recognizable triggers: a slow site, a platform migration, a store integration, or a product needing custom software. Define entry-price scope so a website starting price does not imply a software-project price.

## Naturaily

**Clutch tagline:** “Custom Headless Websites & Shopify Storefronts”

- **Opening and offer:** Identifies a Poland-based web/commerce company, then moves into website, SaaS, and storefront delivery. Its menu connects frameworks and headless CMSs with Shopify builds, custom commerce apps, migrations, and integrations; buyer sectors follow.
- **Qualification:** Minimum project $25,000+, $50–$99/hour; the page also displays separate monthly design, development, and commerce packages.
- **Proof and CTA:** Profile claims include experience, 200+ projects, ISO certification, platform partnerships, and awards. A closing invitation to discuss work sits alongside Clutch's standard website/contact/proposal/question actions. [Naturaily on Clutch](https://clutch.co/profile/naturaily)

**DesignRush tagline:** “Custom web development and fast storefronts”

- **Opening and offer:** Explains why teams seek help: difficult editing, slow changes, and fragmented content workflows. Connects performance and CMS architecture to business integrations, Shopify work, and ongoing maintenance.
- **Proof and CTA:** Names projects and claims outcomes in the description; portfolio cards include scopes, dates, durations, and budgets. Certification/partner claims and separate review-source labels add further evidence layers. In addition to website/contact actions, the description offers a CMS selection report. Budget $25,000–$50,000; $99/hour. [Naturaily on DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/profile/naturaily)

**PandaCodeGen lesson (inference):** Name specific integrations and maintenance deliverables instead of relying on generic business-growth language. Use an educational CTA only when a useful asset exists; a concise project-fit conversation is sufficient otherwise.

## Blazity

**Clutch tagline:** “Experts in building AI agents & workflows”

- **Opening and offer:** Leads with enterprise AI delivery on Vercel, then names uncertainty about AI scope as the buying problem. The process progresses from operational assessment to design, implementation, and maintenance. Technical assets include migration automation and internal workflow tooling.
- **Qualification:** Minimum project $10,000+, $50–$99/hour; web and custom software each occupy half of the listed service mix.
- **Proof and CTA:** The profile claims a Vercel partnership, engineering tenure, a proprietary toolkit, and open-source assets, including a GitHub-star count. A one-call diagnosis invitation complements standard Clutch actions. None of those asset claims were independently audited here. [Blazity on Clutch](https://clutch.co/profile/blazity)

**DesignRush tagline:** “Blazity Software - Empowering creators with the latest technology.”

- **Opening and offer:** Describes a small Warsaw software company focused on mobile/web work for corporate and startup clients. It ends with a general invitation to discuss the business.
- **Proof and CTA:** Explicitly labeled unclaimed. Visible reviews are sourced from Google; client fields name sectors rather than actual clients. Website/contact actions remain available. Budget $10,000–$25,000; $99/hour. This page's published positioning differs materially from Clutch. [Blazity Software on DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/profile/blazity-software)

**PandaCodeGen lesson (inference):** Give uncertain buyers a concrete first step: share the current site/store, problem, and intended scope. Distinguish AI SEO from AI-agent development. Include every live listing in maintenance, including unclaimed ones.

## FocusReactive

**Clutch tagline:** “Succeeding in critical tasks that requires focus”

- **Opening and offer:** The description is more specific than its tagline: engineering-led content systems, headless CMS, Next.js, and integrations. It explains editorial independence, localization, migration continuity, audits, and ongoing governance. Integration categories include CRM, search, analytics, and business systems.
- **Qualification:** Minimum project $10,000+, $50–$99/hour; web, software, design, and commerce categories.
- **Proof and CTA:** Claims multiple CMS partnerships and proprietary implementation tools; Clutch displays verification and review modules. The description links to its own contact section alongside platform contact/proposal actions. [FocusReactive on Clutch](https://clutch.co/profile/focusreactive)

**DesignRush tagline:** “Scalable Headless CMS Solutions. Across code, content, and teams.”

- **Opening and offer:** Connects engineering, commerce, and CMS migration with maintainable content systems. Explicitly includes AI SEO, editorial workflows, and performance in project delivery, with independent publishing as the buyer benefit.
- **Proof and CTA:** Names partner platforms; portfolio cards specify migrations/integrations, budgets, durations, and years. Client lists and engineer biographies add specificity. Website/contact actions; the website link points to a relevant service page. Pricing fields invite inquiry rather than stating numbers. [FocusReactive on DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/profile/focusreactive)

**PandaCodeGen lesson (inference):** Connect implementation details to practical client tasks, then show work that supports those claims. A focused service-page destination can preserve context when a listing centers on one offer.

## Recommendations for the founder-led studio

These are editorial recommendations derived from the patterns above, not measured performance findings:

1. **Make the buying categories easy to separate.** State websites/stores from **$1,500**, custom software quoted after scope, and ongoing AI SEO from **$500/month** as three distinct commercial offers. Explain the minimum website/store scope and what affects price.
2. **Open with the client and the deliverable.** Founder-led delivery and specific website, commerce, software, and monthly SEO work are more usable inputs than an unsupported agency-size or market-leadership claim.
3. **Organize technical services by the problem.** Put speed work, migrations, and integrations under the actual situations that trigger them. Name relevant platforms only where the studio can substantiate the experience.
4. **Use owned evidence.** Link identifiable work, describe what was built, and attach dated metrics only when their baseline and measurement method exist. Do not import competitor partnerships, review-derived results, or directory awards into PandaCodeGen's credibility claims.
5. **End with one workable action.** Invite a project brief or existing URL plus required scope. For AI SEO, clarify that the next step concerns an ongoing monthly engagement, without promising rankings or AI citations.
6. **Keep a field-level record.** Maintain one canonical name, tagline, opening, offer/pricing wording, service categories, proof links, and CTA. Track directory-specific fields and update dates so older listings do not describe a different business.

No GoodFirms sources were needed: both requested priority directories supplied usable pages for all five agencies.
