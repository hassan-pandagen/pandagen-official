## Recommended decision

Keep the existing URL and its build-versus-hire question. Replace the answer implied by **“Yes, Until It Has a Job.”** Business enquiries, bookings and payments do not inherently make DIY uneconomic: hosted platforms supply standard business workflows, and buying a custom implementation introduces its own build and maintenance costs. The useful dividing factors are scope, capability fit, owner time, operational responsibilities and the actual quote.

Recommended H1/title: **Is It Cheaper to Build Your Own Website? DIY vs Hiring**.

Recommended description: **Compare DIY website costs with hiring a developer: first-year bills, renewal fees, your time and when a small business should pay for help.**

Recommended opening answer: **Usually, building it yourself costs less upfront when a platform's existing features cover what you need. Hiring can make sense when your time, design requirements or custom workflows justify the fee. Compare the same scope over a year, with cash costs and your own hours shown separately.**

Do not change this into “best AI website builder” or a comprehensive generic website-cost guide. The first requires comparative product testing and the second already has a separate route. AI changes the implementation options within this decision; it does not replace the reader's decision.

## Suggested article flow

1. Direct answer and transparent disclosure that PandaCodeGen sells development.
2. Short decision table: DIY, targeted help, commissioned build. Cover genuine small-business cases, not only hobbies.
3. Same-scope cash comparison: setup, annual software/hosting, domain, optional tools and support. Name what is included and excluded.
4. A simple first-year equation and owner-time break-even illustration. Clearly label every assumed input.
5. Renewal and ongoing-responsibility checklist, with cash and time separate.
6. What AI builders change and what the owner still needs to decide. Short and fairly sourced, not a seven-product roundup.
7. When hiring or partial assistance makes sense; questions to make quotes comparable.
8. Current PandaCodeGen scope/price in a small disclosed supplier box; one founder-contact CTA.
9. FAQs only for supplementary questions, plus source/check date.

An illustrative model can be useful without becoming invented evidence. Example method: **first-year cash = setup + payable platform/hosting charges + domain + selected tools + agreed support + applicable transaction/usage fees**. Add owner hours separately, including briefing and review on the hired side. Economic comparison = cash + hours × the owner's chosen time value. A break-even calculation shows a choice under its assumptions; it does not establish that the owner can sell every released hour, that DIY is less reliable, or that a hired site will generate more sales.

Do not use made-up repair, missed-lead or sales uplift figures to force a hired option to win. Show at least one reasonable scenario where DIY remains the cheaper answer. If annual subscriptions are payable upfront, make that cash commitment visible. If a promotional rate requires several years prepaid, do not show only its monthly equivalent as the initial bill.

## Content to remove or move

- Remove the claim that real business workflows are a boundary beyond which DIY stops being cheaper.
- Remove all universal “all costs arrive after launch” and “the build was never expensive” language. Setup, integration design, content and testing occur before launch; build cost can dominate a project.
- Remove the 37/48 statistic from this cost article and its blog-card hook. This selected sample does not establish an AI-builder failure rate or a causal cost disadvantage, and an accessibility-tree audit is not the rule every AI assistant uses to determine readability.
- Remove the AI-referral-revenue detour. It does not compare DIY and hired costs or establish that a commissioned build caused assistant-referred sales.
- Remove generic plugin vulnerability stories unless a narrow maintenance example is indispensable. They distract from cost decisions and can become unfair platform fear.
- Replace the fixed 5–7-page/$1,500/2–4-week promise with current canon scope and starting-price language. Do not imply maintenance is included in the build fee.
- Remove competitor minimum-project figures from the main comparison unless researching and comparing actual equivalent scopes. A large agency's entry threshold is not a like-for-like alternative to a small-site quote.
- Fix CTA destination mismatch: copy inviting a URL/question should open contact, not a software-service page labelled as a different next step.
