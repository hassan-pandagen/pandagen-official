## What the comparison supports

The entry market includes materially different products: continuing a sound foundation, local SEO execution, content/specification delivery with separate development, and technically focused maintenance. **“Others charge $1,500–$8,000” is too broad to justify PandaCodeGen's price advantage.** A cheaper quote can buy less content, fewer markets, a smaller agreed workload or no deployment. A high published price does not establish the scope is superior either.

The useful common pattern is a baseline, a defined monthly work allowance and an understandable report. The useful difference PandaCodeGen can offer is engineering implementation within the agreed project scope. A retainer should say what gets changed, not merely what gets checked. Competitor article counts and local-directory quotas are examples of how they bound work; they are not mandatory deliverables or proof of value.

## Recommended structure for a $500 starting engagement

This is a proposed operating model for owner review, not an approved package, capacity promise or statement of existing practice. Quote against **project size, complexity and agreed monthly scope**, not a flat charge per website. Page count is one workload input: five complex templates can require more work than fifty simple pages.

A focused starting example could cover five agreed priority pages, ten buyer questions on two selected AI-search surfaces, one monthly human review and one implemented page improvement or small technical task. Include a concise monthly report of changes shipped, observations, referral/enquiry evidence where available, and next priorities. Name the surfaces and sampling conditions rather than saying “all AI engines.” These quantities are proposed boundaries, not competitor-derived standards.

Define the implementation unit before selling it. An update to one existing service page using approved facts is a different job from researching a new article, changing a template across the catalogue or repairing an integration. State the allowed review round, approval owner, access dependencies and what happens if the client's team cannot supply source facts. “One task” without a complexity boundary can become an unlimited promise.

Baseline and monthly work should be separated. The initial check may discover that the selected pages are inaccessible, tracking is missing or the required changes exceed the recurring allowance. Put substantial remediation in an agreed project scope before work begins; if included in the build, do not charge the client again for that same completed work. No new universal setup fee is implied by this recommendation.

For larger projects, the quote should show which additions drive the fee: more templates or priority pages, additional markets/languages, research-heavy content, more testing surfaces/frequency, integrations, stakeholder reviews or a larger implementation backlog. A client should be able to see why their quote is higher than the floor without being placed into an arbitrary “large site” bracket.

Before publishing, confirm that the example fits PandaCodeGen's actual delivery capacity and margin. An internal estimate of four to six hours would be a planning assumption until a pilot records real effort; it should not become a customer entitlement by accident. Specify the chosen term, cancellation notice, unused-allowance treatment and handover in the written quote. The research does not justify inventing a minimum commitment.

Suggested pricing sentence: **“Monthly AI SEO support starts at $500. We quote for your project's size, complexity and agreed scope, including which pages we work on, what we implement and how we report progress.”** Follow it with one honest example, then explain that larger programmes are scoped separately. Keep placement, traffic and revenue guarantees out of the proposed offer; commit to reviewable work and evidence instead.
