# Monthly AI SEO offer recommendation

**Recommended offer: a scoped monthly service that researches buyer questions, monitors relevant AI-search visibility, implements agreed improvements and explains the results.** Keep the entry price at **$500/month**, with the final quote determined by project size, complexity and the agreed work. The starting fee is for an engagement, not an automatic charge for each website.

PandaCodeGen's established strengths are technical implementation, website/content systems, measurement and founder involvement. Those strengths can support a useful recurring service. The offer should also include attention to what customers need to know: a technically readable page can still omit the facts that make it useful to a buyer.

The quantities and operating terms recommended below are **proposed scope**, not a statement that an existing customer has purchased them or that they are already published. The working capacity assumption is four to six total delivery hours per entry engagement each month. Actual labour cost and available capacity have not been confirmed; the pilot should test them.

## What other companies publish

The comparison includes ten managed-service offers across larger agencies and smaller specialists, checked on 15 September 2026. These are vendor descriptions, not independently verified delivery or results. Original currencies are retained and setup/commitment differences matter.

| Quint-IA Vantage | Two monthly content pieces and visibility reporting | $950/month; technical deployment costs extra; required $500 Blueprint with conditional first-month credit.[^2] |

| Visionary | Content, light implementation and reporting within integrated SEO/GEO | Published £1,300–£2,500 range; onboarding and ongoing production need distinguishing.[^5] |

The [established-agency matrix](established-agency-offers.md) also covers Primo Interactive, Omnius and iPullRank. The [specialist comparison](specialist-entry-offers.md) adds East Kent SEO and ScopeSite and records setup fees, term lengths, implementation boundaries and missing details.

The useful pattern is an initial baseline followed by a recurring cycle of research, work and reporting. The differences are how much content gets produced, whether development is included, how many markets or questions are covered, and how the engagement is staffed. A software dashboard, a list of recommendations and changes implemented on a website are distinct deliverables. An apparently similar price can buy a very different service.

These offers do not establish an industry-standard article quota or a universal price. In particular, local business listings, review support and city-specific targeting do not automatically transfer to a national ecommerce project. Borrow the clarity of scope rather than every tactic.

## The offer to build

Use the commercial category **AI SEO services**, with a plain explanation of the work:

> We research the questions your buyers ask, monitor relevant AI-search results, and make agreed improvements to your content and technical setup. Each month you receive a record of the work completed, what we observed and the next priorities.

The agency's responsibility should be to convert findings into useful changes within the agreed scope. Monitoring helps decide what to work on; it is not the whole product. A small entry engagement should still include an implemented improvement rather than leave every finding for the customer to resolve.

The delivery options can accommodate an existing site, a new PandaCodeGen build or collaboration with an internal team. Before accepting implementation work, confirm access and competence with the actual platform. A handoff to the client's developer must be explicitly agreed if PandaCodeGen will not publish the change itself. Do not sell a delivered change and silently replace it with a ticket.

## Proposed focused engagement from $500/month

This is a concrete minimum-scope example to test. The five-page focus is the work area, not a maximum size for the customer's whole business or a per-page tariff. A larger business can commission a narrow project; a small-looking project can still need a higher quote when the work is complex.

| Included element | Proposed entry boundary | What the customer receives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer and competitor research | One agreed product/service area, one primary market and language; up to five priority pages; ten buyer questions; up to three comparison businesses | A named focus and prioritised questions grounded in the business's customers and available external research |
| AI-search monitoring | Ten agreed questions on two named surfaces, with a monthly human review | Dated observations, linked/cited sources where present and changes in the selected comparison set |
| Search and technical review | Monthly review of the selected pages, available Search Console/referral data and relevant access/indexing/structure issues | A short priority list explaining which issues need action and which do not |
| Implemented improvement | One bounded existing-page improvement, **or** one small technical task agreed for the cycle; one consolidated review round | A shipped change and verification, using client-approved facts and supported access |
| Reporting | One concise monthly report | Work completed, measurements with their limits, obstacles and next priorities |
| Client review | One 20-minute review or recorded walkthrough, plus routine clarification | A clear next decision without requiring a long meeting |

The monitoring contract should name the surfaces, language, country and frequency. Automated collection can run more often than the human review where the licensed tool supports it; do not sell daily expert analysis merely because software collects daily. If collection is manual, state the sampling cadence and its limitations explicitly.

Ten questions is a proposed scope boundary, not a statistically representative survey of all searches. Twenty observations from ten questions on two surfaces are not twenty customers and cannot establish a platform-wide share of voice. Preserve the question set and collection conditions so differences are interpretable; label changes to the panel rather than blending them into a growth claim.

### Define the implemented improvement

Internally, plan approximately **two hours including implementation, validation and the stated revision** for this part of the entry scope. This is a capacity check, not hourly billing or a public entitlement to arbitrary development. Define the actual deliverable in the quote, then choose monthly work that fits it.

Examples that may fit, subject to inspection:

- Improve an existing service page's explanation of process, fit, price basis or FAQs using facts supplied and approved by the client; add appropriate internal links and validate the result.
- Clarify important product information on one selected existing page, using approved specifications, ordering constraints and lead-time information.
- Correct a small, well-understood indexing, metadata or structured-data issue and verify the affected page/template. Template changes require their actual impact to be checked before calling them small.

A new research-heavy article, a new catalogue template, checkout work or an unfamiliar integration is not automatically one small task. One deliverable should not become a container for unlimited effort. Conversely, a broken canonical and a minor link correction can be handled together when they fit the agreed improvement rather than being artificially counted as separate paid projects.

Content relevance should remain available inside the entry scope. A new article every month is not required to create value, but repeatedly checking crawlers while useful commercial information stays missing would be a weak service. Google continues to emphasise useful original content alongside technical fundamentals and says that special AI markup or llms.txt is not a Search-ranking shortcut.[^6]

## What happens in the first month

An entry project with sound foundations should be able to begin at the published starting fee. Do not make an undisclosed setup payment compulsory for everyone.

The proposed first month establishes access, target pages, customer questions, baseline observations and a short prioritised backlog. Include one modest implemented improvement and the first report when the initial inspection confirms this fits the minimum scope. Explain that the first report establishes the baseline; it does not yet demonstrate a reliable trend or causal result.

If the initial inspection reveals substantial work—missing measurement infrastructure, a difficult deployment process, a large technical backlog, multiple languages or a rebuild—quote a defined initial project before accepting the monthly commitment. Separate that initial work from the recurring fee in the proposal. Do not promise normal ongoing output and a major setup project for the same entry price.

For a new PandaCodeGen build, use the existing handover checks as the starting baseline. Work already included in the build, or corrections owed under its accepted terms, should remain part of those obligations. The retainer pays for the subsequent agreed cycle and new improvements.

## What repeats after onboarding

Each monthly cycle should answer five questions:

1. What relevant buyer question or business change needs attention?
2. What did the current data and observations show?
3. Which improvement is worth doing within the agreed scope?
4. What was implemented, approved and verified?
5. What should happen next, including whether continued monthly work is warranted?

This creates recurring work even when HTML rendering is already correct: product or service facts change, new buyer objections emerge, useful pages need refinement, measurement reveals gaps and releases can introduce regressions. It does not justify manufacturing edits every month. At an approximately 90-day review, recommend continuing, changing scope, reducing frequency or stopping according to the remaining useful work and evidence. Ninety days is a review point, not a promise of rankings or a mandatory contract term.

## What needs a larger quote

Quote the workload of the project, not the client's perceived ability to pay or a formula multiplying the number of sites. Specify the additions that change effort:

| Scope change | How it affects the quote |
| --- | --- |
| More priority journeys, pages or templates | Additional investigation, editing and verification |
| More languages or markets | Local research, source review, approval and monitoring requirements |
| More original content | Expert interviews, research, drafting, fact checking, revisions and publication |
| More implementation | A larger agreed backlog or more complex engineering work |
| Ecommerce operations | Product data, feeds, categories, variants and integration requirements where explicitly included |
| More platforms/questions or higher review frequency | Tool costs and analyst time, not merely a larger dashboard |
| Internal team coordination | Additional meetings, approvals, deployment reviews or procurement requirements |
| Multiple brands or properties | Their actual shared and separate work, assessed as one project scope rather than an automatic per-site fee |

For mid-sized projects, keep the same research-to-implementation cycle and expand its capacity and responsibilities. If the client needs substantial original content, PR, multi-market coordination and technical development, provide a correspondingly scoped quote. A $500 starting price should never imply that every programme costs $500.

## Boundaries and client responsibilities

For the entry example, quote separately for new long-form content, extensive copy research, migrations/redesigns, major integrations, custom dashboards, extensive analytics repair, paid placements and ongoing outreach campaigns. A particular addition can be included in a larger retainer once it is costed. Do not offer guaranteed citations, paid-review schemes or a fixed number of acquired links simply because a competitor lists them.

The client provides access, accurate business facts, a decision maker and consolidated approval. If the client's deployment team must release the work, record that responsibility and what counts as completed delivery. Set a response deadline in the actual work plan; do not quietly treat unapproved edits as published results.

For a request that is too large, offer three clear choices before starting: reprioritise within the existing scope, agree a separately priced project, or expand the retainer. Do not surprise the customer with an invoice after exceeding scope. Do not silently replace a promised deliverable with a large task that consumes multiple cycles.

Recommended commercial direction: rolling monthly billing with a plainly stated cancellation procedure, an explicit treatment of deferred work and client access to the completed reports/content/configuration. Set these terms in the written proposal. Avoid automatic loss of paid work when the agency causes a delay, and avoid unlimited rollover that creates an unstaffed backlog. Client-owned accounts should remain under client control; agency software subscriptions require a clear export/handover arrangement.

## Delivery economics and tooling

Begin with a four-to-six-hour planning envelope and measure total time, including reporting, calls, revisions and administration. This is provisional; it must not be presented as proven profitability or silently turned into a customer-facing hours package.

Illustrative monthly contribution, using **$50 in allocated tools and 3% in collection costs** ($15 at a $500 fee), plus hypothetical loaded delivery costs:

| Hours | Loaded hourly cost | Labour + allocation | Remaining from $500 | Contribution percentage |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | $25 | $165 | $335 | 67% |
| 5 | $40 | $265 | $235 | 47% |
| 6 | $60 | $425 | $75 | 15% |
| 10 | $40 | $465 | $35 | 7% |

The figures are assumptions, not PandaCodeGen's costs, actual payment-provider fees or net profits. Any overhead, sales effort, tax or other costs not included in the inputs remain outside the calculation. The [capacity review](delivery-capacity-review.md) explores delivery choices in more detail. With collection fees expressed as a percentage of revenue, a useful quote model is `(expected delivery hours × loaded hourly cost + allocated fixed non-labour costs) ÷ (1 − collection-fee rate − target contribution margin)`. Honour the accepted scope while using observed effort to price future work correctly.

Keep software spending proportionate. OtterlyAI publicly lists Lite at **$29/month for 15 prompts, four named engines and daily collection**, with one workspace; Standard is **$189/month for 100 prompts** and unlimited workspaces. Selected engines are extra add-ons, and the cheaper annual-equivalent prices require annual billing.[^7] These are software offers, not evidence of improved client outcomes. Check the required coverage and agency licence terms before purchase; do not assume one entry subscription can serve an unlimited portfolio.

Use current tools where they fit. A low-cost tracker can collect observations, but someone still needs to select relevant questions, interpret them and implement the work. The proposed retainer's value is that ongoing responsibility. No new tool subscription is required by this document.

## A practical monthly report

Keep the report short enough that an owner will read it. A useful format is:

- **Completed:** agreed change, affected URLs, approval/publication date and verification.
- **Observed:** selected AI-answer samples, relevant Google AI impressions, referrals and qualified enquiries/orders where measurable.
- **Interpretation:** what can reasonably be inferred, data gaps and anything that changed in the sampling or tracking.
- **Next:** the next recommended improvement, client input needed and any scope change requiring a quote.

Google's Generative AI Search report counts impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and page-table aggregation can differ from chart totals.[^8] Keep those observations separate from non-Google prompt samples, visits and business outcomes. A report should remain useful when visibility is flat or there are no measurable leads: explain the work and the next decision rather than manufacturing a positive growth narrative.

## Proposed page copy

Publish only after adopting a scope the team can deliver. The following is draft wording for that chosen service, not a record of an existing contract:

> **Monthly AI SEO services from $500.** We quote based on your project's size and scope, with the work and monthly fee agreed before we begin.
>
> We research the questions your buyers ask, monitor relevant AI-search results, and make agreed improvements to your content and technical setup. Each month, you receive a record of the work completed, what we observed and our recommended next steps.
>
> A focused starting engagement can cover five priority pages, ten agreed buyer questions across two selected AI-search platforms, one implemented page improvement or small technical task, and a monthly report and review. Your written scope names the coverage, deliverables and implementation responsibilities. Larger projects are quoted for the work they need.
>
> Substantial initial repairs, new content production and larger development work are quoted separately where required. We agree objectives and deliverables; we do not guarantee rankings, AI citations or sales.

The public page can show this example without creating Bronze/Silver/Gold tiers. Once adopted, align any existing exclusion of copywriting with the actual distinction between included edits to existing content and separately commissioned original production. Keep the optional-retainer language in metadata, related-service descriptions and llms.txt consistent with the final offer.

## Recommended next action

Test this focused scope on the first one or two suitable engagements. Record effort and completed outputs for two monthly cycles, then adjust the minimum scope or quote method using actual delivery costs. Keep $500 as an achievable starting engagement and use larger quotes for larger projects. Avoid promising a broad growth programme until its research, content, implementation and reporting capacity are established.
