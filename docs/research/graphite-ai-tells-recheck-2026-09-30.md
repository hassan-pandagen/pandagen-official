# Graphite AI tells: editorial recheck

**Recorded:** 30 September 2026  
**Scope:** PandaCodeGen's 88 `/blog/*` articles and other public marketing copy  
**Status:** Recheck when Graphite publishes the Claude Opus 5.5 terms and frames.

## Sources and publication state

- [Graphite's AI Tells study](https://graphite.io/five-percent/research/ai-tells) is the primary source. As checked on 30 September, its public study and downloadable data describe nine models, including Claude Opus 5, but do not include Opus 5.5.
- [VentureBeat's report on Claude Opus 5.5](https://venturebeat.com/data/claude-opus-5-5-uses-em-dashes-99-less-often-and-sounds-more-human-but-still-exhibits-2-548-ai-writing-tells) reports 2,548 overrepresented patterns and says Graphite plans to publish the updated study on Thursday, 1 October. This is advance reporting, not yet a public 2,548-item list we can audit against.

Graphite compares articles written on aligned topics and measures how often terms or sentence frames appear in each corpus. A match is a prompt for an editor to read the passage. It is not proof that a particular page was written by a model. Its corpus and thresholds also limit how well the results transfer to our sales pages, technical guides, and quoted source material.

## What we have done

- Earlier targeted copy work removed or rewrote conspicuous AI-style phrasing in **nine files**, four of them blog articles. See the relevant diffs and the [25 September blog language review](blog-language-review-2026-09-25.md) for a separate, earlier seven-article plain-English sweep.
- The 30 September blog layout pass changed the opening structure of all **88** article routes. It did **not** review every sentence in those articles or complete a sitewide prose edit.
- The current Graphite study informed the editorial checklist below. We have **not** screened against the unpublished Opus 5.5 dataset.

## Recheck procedure after publication

1. Check Graphite's study and data download for an explicit **Claude Opus 5.5** model row. Record the publication date, file name, and methodology version here. Do not infer an update from the VentureBeat headline alone.
2. Extract **visible prose** from the 88 rendered article routes and public marketing pages. Exclude code, JSON-LD, URLs, citations, product names, quoted passages, and hidden UI text from phrase counts.
3. Search the new terms and sentence frames, preserving page URL and surrounding paragraph for each match. Prioritize openings, summaries, calls to action, and repeated page templates.
4. Edit passages only where the writing is vague, repetitive, inflated, or mechanically phrased. Replace abstraction with the concrete actor, action, condition, or example. Preserve the original claim strength, qualification, evidence, and meaning.
5. Have a person read revised passages in page order. Re-run the site's claim, type, lint, and route checks. Record pages reviewed, passages changed, and source version in a dated follow-up file.

## Existing editorial checks

Watch for unspecific evaluative adjectives, formal transitions, definition by contrast, benefit claims that omit tradeoffs, and sentences that announce their own importance. Also check repeated sentence shape and long inventories of abstract nouns. These are **review cues**, not forbidden words. Keep precise technical terms, useful comparisons, natural punctuation, and honest caveats where they help the reader.
