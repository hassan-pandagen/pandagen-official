# Business-facing agency homepages: positioning benchmark

Reviewed 15 September 2026. Primary homepage text, heading markup and linked destinations were inspected for Webstacks, Huemor, Orbit Media and Lounge Lizard. Lounge Lizard substitutes for Bop Design: Bop's available extract omits its hero, direct HTML returned 403, and no interactive browser was available. Its H1 is therefore not guessed.

Placement below means source/document order, not a verified desktop or mobile fold. Some buttons depend on JavaScript; their behaviour is marked unverified where a destination could not be established. No form was submitted. This comparison establishes messaging choices, not that any headline caused better conversion or that an agency's advertised results are independently validated. Verbatim excerpts stay below 25 words per domain.

## The candid answer for PandaCodeGen

The current headline describes one route into the business, not the business buyers can hire. A visitor needing a new company website, an ecommerce storefront or an operations tool can reasonably wonder whether a migration-only agency is relevant. Naming three source platforms in the H1 makes that ambiguity worse: it allocates scarce opening space to qualification details while leaving the useful outcome and the broader offer unstated.

The answer is not an empty slogan about growth or digital transformation. The homepage still needs recognisable service words. Put **website design and development** in the category, connect it to something the buyer wants, and use the next sentence to explain ecommerce and custom software. Platform names remain useful lower down, where someone can find their specific migration path.

## Four current examples

### Webstacks

**H1:** “Composable Web Agency.” A nearby benefit heading says “Future-proof websites that grow with your business.” Supporting copy connects architecture and frameworks to continued delivery. The audience is marketing teams, especially technology businesses. “Talk to an expert” is configured as a popup in the hero's public HTML; the same navigation label links to [sales](https://www.webstacks.com/sales). Project and retainer links separately lead to [redesigns](https://www.webstacks.com/solutions/website-redesigns) and [website product teams](https://www.webstacks.com/solutions/website-product-teams). Logos immediately follow the opening; named client stories precede the broader expertise section. Framework and CMS names appear lower in expertise, examples and navigation. The business remains specific through its audience and engagement models, not a list of platforms in the H1. [Homepage](https://www.webstacks.com/)

### Huemor

**H1:** “B2B Digital Agency.” Its larger benefit heading is “A Great Website Is Just the Beginning.” The lead expands into brand distinction, customer experience and qualified enquiries. “Book A Strategy Session” leads to a [contact/request page](https://huemor.rocks/contact/), not directly to a booked calendar slot. “Instant Website Estimate” leads to a [multistep calculator](https://huemor.rocks/resources/website-cost-calculator/) that requests contact details. A client-logo section follows the opening. Industry fit and three service paths—rebuild, support and search visibility—come next, then result claims and named work. Platform links sit in the footer rather than the hero. Its FAQ describes typical projects around $50,000–$100,000, so its sales process is not automatically suitable for PandaCodeGen's $1,500 entry offer. [Homepage](https://huemor.rocks/)

### Orbit Media

**H1:** “Award-winning digital marketing and web design agency.” Its lead begins “Get a website that works for you.” The remaining copy combines discoverability, easy editing, commercial purpose and brand expression. “Schedule a discovery call” leads to a [contact form](https://www.orbitmedia.com/contact/), which requests identity and needs before a call; it is not immediate scheduling. Work imagery and a scale claim follow the opening, then service detail. Testimonials appear beside subsequent arguments about measurement and delivery. Design, development and ongoing optimisation are the main service categories. The inspected hero does not list CMS names. Its useful specificity is the customer's ability to manage the result, with supporting explanations later. Awards, scale and revenue claims remain the supplier's claims. [Homepage](https://www.orbitmedia.com/)

### Lounge Lizard

**H1:** “New York Web Design Company & Digital Marketing Agency Since 1998.” The lead connects design, development and marketing to brand presentation, enquiries and revenue. “Request Growth Strategy” is a button; its exact opening behaviour could not be verified. The homepage includes consultation forms, and the navigation has a verified [contact destination](https://www.loungelizard.com/contact/). The opening is followed by another agency description, then client-labelled result cards and work navigation. Detailed service categories include websites, ecommerce, development and marketing; named platforms occur lower in those service lists. This is broad positioning supported by explicit service categories and an established-company story. The long opening paragraph and overlapping descriptions are not patterns PandaCodeGen needs to copy. [Homepage](https://www.loungelizard.com/)

## What to adopt, without copying their positioning

**Make the category immediately understandable.** A small business owner should not have to decode architecture vocabulary to establish that PandaCodeGen builds websites. A mid-size marketing manager should also recognise that the team can handle the operational details behind the design. One plain category line can serve both; the proof and service paths then add depth.

**Give the buyer a tangible benefit.** More sales is attractive but not wholly controlled by a development agency. Better product information, a site the staff can update, checkout that fits the order process, clearer service presentation and connected business systems are more concrete discussion points. Choose the benefit the delivered work can demonstrate. A broad outcome headline needs a specific supporting sentence to prevent it becoming interchangeable agency copy.

**Show actual work early.** Place two or three relevant project previews close to the hero. State the business, what was built and the useful change. A screenshot plus a concise account of the job is stronger than an unexplained technology logo. Keep founder-affiliated projects labelled. Do not borrow another agency's impressive-looking metric treatment unless the corresponding PandaCodeGen result has a retrievable baseline, period and definition.

**Offer understandable paths rather than a giant menu.** The first service overview can distinguish company websites, ecommerce and custom business software. Redesign and migration then belong within the website paths. That prevents a buyer with an existing platform from losing their route, while giving a new-build customer and an operations buyer an equally clear entry point. Keep the deeper technical services available for buyers who already know what they need.

**Explain independence in customer language.** Ownership is valuable when it tells someone what they can do after launch: edit content, access accounts, retain the code and appoint another developer. Avoid suggesting that a custom application never requires technical maintenance. The useful proposition is a documented handover and clear responsibilities, not freedom from every future cost.

## Recommended hero direction

Use this as a reviewable direction rather than a promise that changing the H1 alone will improve performance:

**Category:** Website design & custom development

**H1:** A better website. Built around your business.

**Supporting copy:** We design and build company websites, ecommerce stores and custom software around how your business works. New builds, redesigns and migrations, with a clear scope and a price agreed before we start.

**Primary action:** Ask us about your site

**Secondary action:** See our work

**Supporting price line:** Website projects from $1,500. Custom software quoted to scope.

This retains the website as the main acquisition offer while making the other work visible. If custom software becomes an equal homepage priority, the primary enquiry label needs to cover that audience too; a site-only button will not fit someone seeking an internal tool. A broader alternative is **Ask us about your business**. That is an editorial choice to test, not a competitor-derived best practice.

The reassurance already agreed in the conversation belongs directly under the enquiry action. The destination should actually accept a brief question and optional URL, rather than immediately require a migration brief or a scheduled call. The portfolio action should take the reader to projects they can inspect. Its job is proof, so avoid inserting a general methodology argument between the click and the work.

An acceptable plainer H1 is **Websites and software built around your business.** It is clearer about breadth, although less focused on the website buyer. Avoid a line so expansive that a reader expects branding, paid advertising or enterprise transformation unless those are real, supported services. Broad does not mean unlimited.

## Where platforms and migration detail belong

After the hero and initial proof, explain the main buying situations. One section can serve people who need a new site, have outgrown the current one, or need systems connected. Place specific platform links inside the existing-site route with truthful direction: moving from a builder, rebuilding a storefront, or retaining a backend while replacing the front end are different jobs.

Keep WordPress, Wix, Squarespace, Webflow, WooCommerce and the relevant destination technology on their actual service pages. Show compatibility or migration expertise when it helps someone decide. Removing a list from the H1 does not require removing it from navigation or useful internal links. Do not turn the pattern observed here into a rule that platforms must never appear above the fold; the issue is whether they explain the offer or unnecessarily narrow it.

The homepage should let three people answer quickly: **Do they build what I need? Have they done credible work? What happens if I contact them?** A category, a specific lead, accessible projects and an honest enquiry route answer those questions. An isolated clever headline does not.

## Review before publishing

Check the new headline and lead against real project scope, especially what the $1,500 floor covers. Read the entire first screen on desktop and mobile; ensure the project preview remains legible and neither button is buried. Ask representative readers to describe the business after a short view without prompting them with platform names. Finally, record enquiry quality and the paths visitors choose after release. Those observations can test whether the broader positioning attracts the intended buyers; this competitor snapshot cannot supply that result in advance.
