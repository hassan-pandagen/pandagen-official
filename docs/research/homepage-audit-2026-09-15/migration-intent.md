# Migration, redesign and conversion: keyword intent

The strongest plain-language description for PandaCodeGen is **build a new website or redesign and rebuild an existing one**. Use **website migration** when explaining how existing content, URLs, products and connected tools move to the replacement. Avoid **convert your website** as the main service label: it leaves the destination and the work unspecified.

This is a search-intent assessment researched on 15 September 2026. It uses fresh external search results and primary provider pages, not PandaCodeGen's existing Search Console queries. No keyword-volume, difficulty or CPC feed was available for this subtask, so this memo does not assign demand numbers, claim that a phrase is easy to rank for, or treat missing results as zero demand. Search results are a qualitative sample, not a controlled US/UK Google ranking export.

## What the terms mean in the sampled results

| Phrase searched | Observed intent and supporting evidence | Homepage decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| website redesign services | Commercial work on an existing website: design, structure, usability and content. ZAG's service page describes a broader engagement than a cosmetic refresh. [ZAG](https://www.zaginteractive.com/services/website-redesigns/website-redesign-services) | Use naturally in a service route or existing-website section. It does not itself welcome a buyer who has no site yet. |
| website migration services | Mixed hosting, CMS/platform and domain moves appeared in the broad search. The generic term does not specify whether a redesign is included. Established hosting providers supply a clear counterexample to treating all migration searches as rebuild demand. [Kinsta](https://kinsta.com/wordpress-hosting/migration/) | Qualify the work with source, destination and what moves. Keep migration below a broader new-build/redesign introduction. |
| WordPress migration services | A substantial meaning is moving a WordPress installation between hosting providers while retaining WordPress. Kinsta and WordPress.com both describe that type of service. [Kinsta](https://kinsta.com/wordpress-hosting/migration/), [WordPress.com](https://wordpress.com/support/request-a-free-migration/) | “WordPress to a custom website” is more precise for the relevant rebuild offer. “WordPress migration” alone may attract a different, lower-scope job. |
| Wix website redesign | Can mean updating a website within Wix. Wix's own guidance offers refurbishment, duplication, hiring a Wix Partner and starting another Wix site. [Wix](https://support.wix.com/en/article/revamping-your-site-5984263) | If the proposed work leaves Wix, say so. A link labelled only “Wix redesign” can create an incorrect expectation of an in-platform design service. |
| Squarespace to custom website | The sample did not establish a distinct high-volume commercial category. Squarespace's export documentation confirms a practical migration constraint: its export carries selected content, not the complete site and styling. [Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206566687-Exporting-your-site) | Useful descriptive language for a relevant buyer, even without proven volume. Explain that the site is rebuilt and agreed content is moved; do not imply a one-click conversion. |
| Shopify headless development | Technical custom-storefront intent. Shopify explicitly supports using its APIs with a separately chosen technology stack. [Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/headless/bring-your-own-stack) | Keep a dedicated ecommerce route. A custom storefront can retain Shopify, so a platform list must not imply every Shopify prospect must leave it. |
| convert website | Ambiguous destination. Broad results included document conversion; adding “to app” produced app-conversion tools and official web-app guidance. [Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/open-as-web-app-iphea86e5236/ios) | Avoid as the primary label. “Rebuild your existing website” explains the intended service more clearly. |
| website conversion services | Mixed technical conversion and conversion-rate improvement. A focused follow-up found commercial CRO services discussing funnels, copy and usability. [WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/digital-marketing/services/conversion-rate-optimization/) | Do not use as a synonym for migration. Buyers may expect work to improve leads or purchases on the current site rather than a replacement build. |

These observations support a vocabulary choice, not a quantitative ranking of opportunities. In particular, the broad migration results included recently crawled small providers making strong claims; those claims were not adopted as proof, price benchmarks or delivery standards. The established providers above were used to verify the material distinctions.

## Recommended way to express the offer

The homepage can welcome both types of buyer in one sentence:

> We design and build new websites and online stores, and redesign and rebuild existing sites around what your business needs next.

That sentence is a copy recommendation, not a claim that this exact wording has measurable keyword demand. The main heading can remain broader and more memorable, with this sentence immediately underneath. There is no need to put every service noun and every supported platform into the H1.

For an existing-site section lower down:

> **Ready to replace your existing website?**
>
> We redesign and rebuild sites from WordPress, Wix, Squarespace and Webflow as custom websites you can edit and own. Before the move, we agree what happens to your content, URLs, forms and connected tools.

“Fast” can be a build objective within the wider offer, but avoid writing that every old website becomes faster merely because it changes platform. Performance depends on the implementation and should be verified against the agreed pages and test conditions. Likewise, transferring URLs and using redirects describes work under your control; “no ranking losses” is an outcome claim that this keyword research cannot justify.

If WooCommerce is included in the same section, introduce it as an existing online store and link to the store-specific scope. Give Shopify an adjacent but separate sentence:

> For Shopify stores, we can assess whether to improve the current setup, build a custom storefront while keeping Shopify, or plan a wider move.

The Shopify distinction is supported by the official architecture and is already consistent with the ecommerce route's current metadata, which covers theme repair, headless storefronts and replatforming. It should not be compressed into a blanket “move off Shopify” promise.

## Make platform links useful

The platform names belong where a visitor can use them to find their situation. A row of names with no explanation would cover vocabulary but do little to establish fit. Use concise links under the existing-site explanation, with accurate destination pages:

| Suggested link label | Existing route verified in the repository | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WordPress website migration | `/services/wordpress-migration` | This is the canonical route; `/services/wordpress` redirects to it. State the custom rebuild destination in the nearby text. |
| Moving from Wix | `/services/wix` | Identifies the direction of travel. |
| Moving from Squarespace | `/services/squarespace` | Identifies the direction of travel. |
| Moving from Webflow | `/services/webflow` | Identifies the direction of travel. |
| WooCommerce website work | `/services/woocommerce` | Keep catalogue and commerce requirements distinct from brochure-site content. Verify the final link label against its rendered scope before implementation. |
| Shopify storefront options | `/services/ecommerce` | Allows repair, custom storefront and wider migration without forcing one architecture. |

The source route files were checked read-only on 15 September 2026. No new service URLs are recommended by this memo, and no application files were changed.

## Research log and limits

Initial external searches covered all eight phrases in the table. Adaptive follow-ups were “convert website”, “website conversion services” with established-provider filters, “WordPress migration services” with Kinsta/WP Engine/WordPress.com filters, “Squarespace exporting your site” on the official help domain, and “convert website to app”. Searches are not evidence that people search each phrase frequently. They help determine what different phrases can lead a buyer to expect.

The following primary pages were opened and read for the relevant passages on 15 September 2026:

- [Kinsta: WordPress migration service](https://kinsta.com/wordpress-hosting/migration/) — hosting migration rather than a required rebuild. Its performance and downtime marketing claims were not used.
- [Wix: Revamping Your Site](https://support.wix.com/en/article/revamping-your-site-5984263) — several options remain within Wix.
- [Squarespace: Exporting your site](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206566687-Exporting-your-site) — limited content export; page updated 12 February 2026.
- [Shopify: Bring your own headless stack](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/headless/bring-your-own-stack) — custom storefront technology can use Shopify commerce APIs.
- [ZAG: Website Designs & Redesign Services](https://www.zaginteractive.com/services/website-redesigns/website-redesign-services) — existing-site redesign is a commercial category encompassing more than visuals.
- [WebFX: Conversion Rate Optimization Services](https://www.webfx.com/digital-marketing/services/conversion-rate-optimization/) — conversion can refer to visitor actions rather than technology conversion. Its revenue, uplift, package price and tool claims were not adopted.
- [Apple: Turn a website into an app in Safari on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/open-as-web-app-iphea86e5236/ios) — an alternative meaning of turning/converting a website.

For a later quantified keyword decision, collect country-separated US and UK estimates for the final candidate phrases, preserving provider, retrieval date, language, reporting period, monthly trend and raw export. Specific long-tail phrases should not be discarded merely because a provider reports no measurable volume. Conversely, a large generic migration number should not be counted as demand for custom Next.js rebuilds.
