# Technical-studio homepage benchmark

**Checked 15 September 2026.** Four current primary homepages were reviewed: Bejamas, Naturaily, FocusReactive and Blazity. Pagepro was requested but could not be fully accessed, so Blazity replaces it in the completed comparison. This is a positioning and information-flow review, not a ranking of agencies or a conversion study. No PandaCodeGen application files changed.

The useful distinction is **who must understand the offer first**. A business owner buying an initial website needs a recognisable service, a relevant example and a cost basis. A technical buyer already choosing an implementation partner may actively want framework and platform names. Neither audience should have to decode copy written primarily for the other.

Evidence comes from readable official pages and direct public HTML. H1 tags and CTA hrefs were checked in the markup where stated. Browser control was unavailable; therefore “near the hero” refers to document/section order, not a measured desktop or mobile fold. No forms were submitted, calendars booked or modal interactions tested. Client logos, testimonials and result figures are the agencies' published proof, not independently validated outcomes.

## Observed pages and destinations

### Bejamas

**H1:** “Migrate to a website your team can run.”

Its lead describes a European team handling legacy-site assessment, migration and continued operation so marketing can publish independently. The apparent buyer is a marketing/digital team with an established, complicated estate. Danone, Rippling and other client logos appear in the hero region. Subsequent sections move through operating problems, services, projects and a client testimonial.

**CTAs:** “Talk to us” links to the [contact page](https://bejamas.com/get-in-touch), containing an enquiry form; “See how we work” links to the [services overview](https://bejamas.com/services), not a calendar or process modal.

Next.js, Astro and CMS names sit in navigation, stack routes and project labels; they are absent from the principal headline. The business consequence leads while the implementation remains discoverable. [Current homepage](https://bejamas.com/)

### Naturaily

**H1, preserving its words:** “YOUR HOW DID YOU FIND THEM WEB DEVELOPMENT & SHOPIFY AGENCY”.

The short lead stresses design and measurable outcomes. Immediate proof includes a published 4.9/5 Clutch rating, experience/project counts and named customer testimonials. A substantial case showcase follows, covering SaaS, ecommerce and operational integrations before the service overview.

**CTA:** “CONTACT US” links to [/get-an-estimate](https://naturaily.com/get-an-estimate), a form collecting contact/company details, project description and service type. It is an enquiry destination, not an instant estimate calculator.

Shopify is in the H1; Next.js, Nuxt and CMS names occur in navigation, case descriptions and a later technology section. Content/AI visibility is a navigation service category rather than the hero's central promise. Its published examples address business and marketing buyers as well as technical delivery. [Current homepage](https://naturaily.com/)

### FocusReactive

**H1:** “Headless CMS Development Company, Sanity, Storyblok & more”. **Larger hero H2:** “Scalable Headless CMS Solutions. Across code, content, and teams.”

The markup distinguishes a category eyebrow from the principal hero title. The lead names Next.js, headless commerce and AI SEO alongside editorial growth and additional markets. A Clutch link sits in the hero; project cards immediately follow with industry, stack and delivery scope.

**Header CTA:** “Contact Us” links to [#contacts](https://focusreactive.com/#contacts), the homepage form. It asks about service, budget and timeframe. No sales CTA appeared inside the inspected hero section itself.

Technology is prominent throughout. Marketing teams managing complex publishing and technical buyers selecting a CMS partner are the apparent audiences. It connects architecture to editorial operations rather than limiting the explanation to framework features. [Current homepage](https://focusreactive.com/)

### Blazity — current substitute

**H1:** “We are experts in building enterprise AI Agents & workflows”.

The lead offers production AI systems on Vercel AI Cloud, including engineering and ongoing operation. A Vercel certification badge and customer logos follow immediately. Its page then explains a delivery system, tools and services before case studies and testimonials.

**CTAs:** “Talk to an architect” points to [#contact](https://www.blazity.com/#contact), an in-page enquiry form; “SEE OUR solutions” opens [/solutions](https://www.blazity.com/solutions).

Vercel appears above the H1 and in the lead. Next.js services remain farther down. The explicit audience is enterprise AI/engineering procurement, with business and developer applications both described. This is not currently a general small-business website pitch, despite overlap in implementation technology. Its prominent platform language belongs to that specific offer. [Current homepage](https://www.blazity.com/)

### Pagepro access boundary

The [Pagepro homepage](https://pagepro.co/) returned only a tag-manager iframe to the web reader; a direct HTML request returned HTTP 403. Its current H1, hero proof and CTA destinations were consequently not established. Accessible articles, search snippets and other companies with similar names were not substituted for homepage evidence. This is an access limitation of this review, not a finding about ordinary customers' experience.

## What PandaCodeGen should take from this

The following recommendations are our interpretation for PandaCodeGen's stated offer: website builds starting at $1,500, ecommerce, custom business software and optional AEO work. They are not competitor conversion findings or newly agreed inclusions.

### Put the purchase category before the implementation decision

A first-time visitor should quickly understand that PandaCodeGen builds websites, stores and business software. Those are different purchases with related engineering behind them. A headline that only names a framework asks a nontechnical buyer to solve a technology-selection problem before recognising the service. A headline that only promises business improvement creates the opposite problem: it could describe an advertising agency, consultant or software vendor.

Use a concrete category in the opening and a short explanation of the customer problem. Keep the technology visible in a supporting sentence, relevant work cards and specialist service links. This preserves relevance for someone specifically searching for a Next.js developer without requiring every business owner to know that term. If a campaign targets CTOs, its landing page can lead with the technical specialism more strongly than the general homepage.

Do not force all three purchases into an elaborate slogan. The homepage needs a clear umbrella and quick routes into the appropriate scope. Platform preferences can be discussed after the visitor recognises their need.

### Make the starting price describe a purchasable boundary

Place the $1,500 starting price beside the website-build offer, with a direct path to what that entry scope covers. Do not make its position imply that an ecommerce catalogue, bespoke CRM, migration and monthly search work all fit the same minimum. An interested owner should understand what changes the quote without first booking a call.

The quote should resolve page/template scope, content responsibilities, integrations, revisions, delivery dependencies, ownership, support and recurring costs. The homepage can link to those details rather than reproduce a contract. Competitive confidence comes from making the purchasing decision understandable, not from comparing an entry build against an unspecified enterprise engagement.

### Let the work establish relevance early

Show a small selection of real projects soon after the opening. Give each a screenshot, business type, customer problem and concise account of what shipped. A relevant website and an operational software example can demonstrate range more clearly than many abstract capability icons.

Retain ownership/affiliation disclosures with affiliated projects. If a measured result is published, state its source and conditions where the reader encounters it. When a defensible numerical outcome is unavailable, the live interface, functionality and documented scope still provide useful evidence. Do not fill that gap with unsourced conversion claims.

The selection should help a visitor answer whether the team has handled a comparable need. It need not imply that the largest-looking project is automatically the most relevant example for a smaller enquiry.

### Match CTA wording to the next screen

A contact label should open a contact route. A call label should reach a functioning scheduler. A promise of an estimate should explain whether the visitor receives it immediately or after review. These expectations matter independently of whether the destination is a modal, dedicated page or in-page form.

For the general homepage, retain an invitation that permits an undeveloped question. Pair it with a work-browsing route so a visitor can evaluate the agency before supplying details. A website-specific invitation can work near website copy; software sections should invite discussion of the workflow or operational problem. Reuse the same destination where appropriate, carrying useful context into the form rather than creating competing enquiry journeys.

Show the already agreed founder-response reassurance close to the action. A visitor should not have to reach the form to discover that asking a question does not commit them to a project.

### Separate AI visibility from AI software

AEO concerns how buyers encounter the business in AI-assisted search. Custom software or agents concern what a system does for the business. Combining them into one broad AI promise makes scope harder to assess.

Treat search readiness as part of the relevant build standards where actually included. Present ongoing research, monitoring and page improvements as their own optional service with its own scope and fee. Keep workflow automation tied to a concrete task, user and integration. Do not let the current market's AI terminology displace the website and software purchases PandaCodeGen already wants to sell.

## How to judge the revision

Use a short comprehension review with likely customers before claiming improvement: ask what the company sells, who it appears to serve, what starts at $1,500, which project is relevant, and what the primary button will do. Check the same journey on a phone. Record misunderstandings and change the relevant copy or order.

After release, evaluate qualified enquiries and service fit alongside CTA use; raw clicks alone do not establish a better homepage. These are proposed checks, not a forecast of conversion lift. The benchmark supplies observable alternatives and clearer design decisions, not proof that copying a larger studio will produce its results.
