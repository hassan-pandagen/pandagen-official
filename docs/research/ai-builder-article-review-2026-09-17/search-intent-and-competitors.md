# AI-built website article: search intent and competing pages

Research date: 17 September 2026. Draft reviewed: `blog-drafts/ai-website-builder-small-business-checklist.md`, v2. This is a search-result and page-content review, not a rank tracker, hands-on comparison of builders, or traffic forecast. No production files changed.

## Decision

Make this a practical verification guide for an owner who already has a website. Its strongest publishable promise is helping that person complete a business-critical journey and record what actually happened. The recurring-cost argument should explain when to repeat the checks, rather than become a second DIY-versus-agency cost article.

Recommended H1: **AI-Built Website Checklist: 8 Checks Before Launch and After Changes**.

Suggested title tag: **AI-Built Website Checklist: 8 Practical Checks**.

Suggested description: **Check that enquiries arrive, payments reconcile and customers can use your AI-built site. Eight practical tests, evidence to save and when to repeat them.**

Suggested short answer: An AI-built business website needs the same live verification as any other website. Complete its main customer journey, check the resulting business record, test failure paths and assign someone to repeat the relevant checks after changes. This guide starts with enquiries and payments; it is not a complete security or accessibility assessment.

Use the existing filename until the owner approves a publication slug. A possible future slug is `/blog/ai-built-website-checklist`; no published URL requires changing for this recommendation.

## What can make the article materially better

An eight-item list is useful, but it is not unique. Produce an **ungated website check record** alongside the article, with fields for:

- Customer action and live URL.
- Test date, environment and site version where available.
- Expected result and actual result.
- Evidence reference: enquiry/order ID, redacted delivery event, receipt screenshot or documented observation.
- Pass, fail, not applicable or not verified. Unknown must not become a pass.
- Responsible person, action required and next test trigger.

The record should work for a nontechnical owner. A contact-form example should show the sequence from visitor confirmation to stored enquiry, sender's delivery status, receiving inbox and any CRM handoff that is actually in scope. Explain why each stage answers a different question. A delivery event alone does not establish inbox placement or human handling.

Use a clearly labelled illustrative completed row until a real redacted test can be supplied. For a stronger version, publish one genuine before/fix/retest sequence with its date, affected scope and limitations. Do not fabricate a screenshot, incident outcome, duration, customer loss or sample size to make it impressive.

The recurring element needs an actionable schedule rather than “someone must keep checking.” Suggested policy, labelled as a starting point to adapt: repeat the affected journey after a related release, integration/configuration change or recipient change; assign a routine cadence based on business impact; use monitoring for critical failures where supported. Do not invent a universal one-to-two-hour maintenance estimate.

Separate owner checks from technical checks. An owner can inspect inbox delivery, navigation and business records. Webhook replay, permission isolation, server error tracing and restore testing may require an authorised developer or vendor. Payment testing should follow the processor's sandbox guidance, with a controlled production smoke test only when appropriate and authorised.

## Recommended reading flow

1. H1, short answer and one-sentence scope.
2. Small applicability table: enquiry site, store, member/software system.
3. Eight-check overview with jump links and a direct link to the reusable check record.
4. Check instructions, each with action, expected result, evidence and what to do if it fails.
5. One or two verified and resolved first-party examples with redacted proof.
6. What today's builders already provide, compressed to a short capability summary with dated vendor sources.
7. When to repeat checks and who owns them.
8. Help CTA and genuinely supplementary FAQs.

Suggested CTA heading: **Not sure whether your site passes?**

Suggested copy: **Tell us what your site should do and which check failed. A founder will reply and explain the next step. Any paid work is scoped and quoted before it starts.**

Suggested button: **Ask a founder about your site**.

This promises a conversation, not an unconfirmed free audit, maintenance SLA, fixed-price repair package or complete security review. Keep the $1,500 new-build price off this article's primary CTA unless the section explicitly discusses commissioning a new build; most readers are checking something they already own.

## Quality bar

A high-quality version is one an owner can use without booking a call. It should not receive a 9/10 solely because the writing is polished or keywords are present. Before assigning that score, have at least one nontechnical owner attempt the checklist and note where they cannot find a setting, cannot interpret a result or cannot finish without help. Resolve those observed difficulties. For a stronger benchmark or comparative article, run a disclosed repeatable protocol across specified projects and publish the limitations; vendor documentation alone cannot substitute for that experiment.

The biggest research-driven change is to replace the claim of an overlooked subject with a demonstrably better execution aid. Existing competitors already describe these failures. PandaCodeGen can make the work easier to perform and verify.
